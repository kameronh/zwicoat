# Peel et al. 2024, ACS Applied Bio Materials (coating durability, lubricity and insertion force)

## Header

- Citation: Peel A, Bennion D, Horne R, Hansen MR, Guymon CA. Photografted Zwitterionic Hydrogel Coating Durability for Reduced Foreign Body Response to Cochlear Implants. ACS Appl. Bio Mater. 2024, 7, 3124-3135. Published April 8, 2024.
- DOI: 10.1021/acsabm.4c00156
- Open access: YES. The first page prints "This article is licensed under CC-BY 4.0" with the Open Access badge. Figures and text can be reused with attribution (credit the authors, give the DOI and licence, say if changed). Check for any third-party material credits before reuse; none are flagged in the pages read.
- Coating chemistry: SBMA (sulfobetaine) and CBMA (carboxybetaine) zwitterionic hydrogels, with PEGMA and HEMA (non-zwitterionic) and PEGDMA as controls, photografted onto medical grade PDMS. Cross-linker is PEGDMA, 0 to 100 percent of a 35 wt% monomer solution.
- Disclosures printed: "The authors declare no competing financial interest."
- Plain-language finding: The coatings stay on and keep their very low friction after weeks in mice, drying out and rewetting, bending, long wear and high loads, and the coated arrays need about half the peak force to enter human cadaver cochleae.
- Supporting Information (free, ACS): Figure S1 (tribometer curves, T10 and T90), Figure S2 (friction versus test speed, 1 to 6 mm/s, no significant difference), and two MP4 videos, insertion of an uncoated and a coated array into explanted cochleae. The supporting files were not available to me; only the main PDF was read.
- Page numbering: PDF page n is printed page 3123 + n. All pages below are printed page numbers. PDF pages 11 and 12 are references.

### Figure 2 (p. 3127): coating after implantation, SEM and confocal

- Shows: SEM cross-sections of six-month implants: A, uncoated PDMS; B to E, CBMA-coated PDMS with 2, 5, 13, 31 percent cross-linker. Confocal z-stacks of 16-day implants soaked in fluorescein: F, uncoated PDMS; G, 10 wt% cross-linked CBMA film.

### Figure 3 (p. 3127): fibrotic capsule, 6 weeks, mice

- Shows: A, H&E of uncoated PDMS; B, H&E of CBMA-coated (5 wt% cross-linker) PDMS; C, capsule thickness plot.
- Conditions: uncoated PDMS versus 5 wt% cross-linked CBMA coated PDMS, 6 weeks subcutaneous in BL/6 mice. Capsule includes both the cell-rich interface and the collagen-rich layer next to it.
- Numbers (exact, text p. 3127): coated capsule "over 60% less" (p < 0.05); p = 0.033 in the figure. Bar means read from figure, approximate: uncoated about 45 micrometres, CBMA about 17 micrometres (about 62 percent lower, derived). Individual points: uncoated about five points from about 30 to about 65; CBMA about three points from about 12 to about 27.
- Error bar type and exact n are not stated in the caption. n looks like 5 uncoated and 3 coated from the plotted points.
- Statistic: p = 0.033, test not named in the caption.
- Units: micrometres.
- Text: cell-rich interface "appeared to bear fewer cells" and the collagen-rich layer "almost entirely absent" (qualitative, p. 3127).

### Figure 4 (p. 3128): coefficient of friction, pristine versus explanted

- Shows: bars for uncoated, CBMA-coated and SBMA-coated PDMS, pristine (stored in PBS) versus explanted after 3 weeks in mice.
- Numbers (read from figure, approximate; absolute coefficient of friction, sapphire probe, PBS): uncoated pristine about 0.25, explanted about 0.27; CBMA pristine about 0.035, explanted about 0.035; SBMA pristine about 0.027, explanted about 0.03. Error bars SEM, uncoated about 0.05.
- Text exact: no significant difference between pristine and explanted; about 90 percent reduction in coefficient of friction versus uncoated for both CBMA and SBMA.
- n: 4 or more per group. Statistic: not named; "no significant difference".
- Units: dimensionless.
- Note: 0.25 to 0.03 is about an 88 percent reduction (about 8 to 9 fold), not the "approximately 20-fold" in the abstract.

### Figure 5 (p. 3128): friction against tissues and across hydrogel types

- Shows: A, relative coefficient of friction for 5 wt% SBMA-coated PDMS against six probes: aorta, bladder, dermis, fat, trachea (all guinea pig tissue-covered probes) and steel, and uncoated PDMS. B, relative coefficient of friction against a dermis-covered probe for different coatings.
- Panel A numbers (read from figure, approximate; uncoated = 1.0 by definition, error about 0.08): aorta about 0.03, bladder about 0.03, dermis about 0.06, fat about 0.02, trachea about 0.05, steel about 0.05. Text exact: at least 90 percent reduction for all tissues; dermis and trachea slightly higher than the others, "similar to that observed simply with the steel probe".
- Panel B numbers (read from figure, approximate, relative to uncoated PDMS on dermis = 1.0): SBMA 2 wt% about 0.04, SBMA 5 wt% about 0.06, SBMA 50 wt% about 0.09, CBMA 5 wt% about 0.08, PEGMA 5 wt% about 0.04, HEMA 5 wt% about 1.15 (error to about 1.5), PEGDMA about 0.57 (error to about 0.95), dermis against dermis about 1.3, uncoated 1.0. Axis labels are slanted and partly cut off; read order carefully.
- Text exact: CBMA and PEGMA, about 95 percent reduction (same as SBMA) at 5 wt% cross-linker; HEMA with 5 wt% cross-linker similar to uncoated PDMS; highly cross-linked PEGDMA reduces by only about 50 percent; dermis on dermis is about 30 percent higher than dermis on uncoated PDMS. The uncoated PDMS value for the dermis probe is 0.301 (absolute, caption).
- n: 3 or more. Error bars SEM. Statistic: not named in the caption.

### Figure 6 (p. 3129): time until friction rises back, versus cross-linker (T10 and T90)

- Shows: A, CBMA; B, SBMA; C, PEGMA. Time (minutes) for the coefficient of friction to reach 10 percent (T10, first signs of drying) and 90 percent (T90, essentially dry) of the total range, versus cross-linker percent. Hydrogels swollen to equilibrium in PBS and then left in ambient air during tribometry.
- Numbers, read from figure, approximate and only rough (error bars are SD, large): at low cross-linker, CBMA T10 about 45 to 50 min and T90 about 60 min or more; SBMA T10 about 30 to 35 min and T90 about 50 to 58 min; PEGMA T10 about 35 to 45 min and T90 about 55 to 65 min. At 100 percent cross-linker all three are about 22 to 23 min (T10) and about 33 min (T90).
- Text exact: CBMA at lower cross-linker, T90 over 60 min; at higher cross-linker, T90 about 30 min. CBMA T10 about 40 min at low cross-link density and as early as 20 min when more cross-linked. SBMA reached its threshold at "up to 60 min" (the text says T10, which looks like a slip given the figure; check). PEGMA T90 up to about 60 min at lower cross-linker. Initial coefficient of friction at 2 wt% cross-linker: PEGMA 0.058, CBMA 0.041, SBMA 0.047.
- n: 3 or more. Units: minutes.

### Figure 7 (p. 3130): time to failure in a mandrel bend test

- Shows: minutes until the film cracked while repeatedly bent every 5 min around a 5 mm mandrel after being allowed to dry in air from the swollen state, versus cross-linker percent, for CBMA, SBMA, PEGMA.
- Numbers, read from figure, approximate: CBMA about 75 min at about 5 percent, about 58 min at about 17 percent, about 25 min at about 67 percent, 0 min at 100 percent. SBMA about 48 min at about 5 percent, about 36 min at about 17 percent, about 7 min at about 67 percent, 0 at 100. PEGMA about 25 min at about 5 percent, about 10 min at about 17 percent, about 5 min at about 67 percent, 0 at 100.
- Text exact: with 5 wt% cross-linker, CBMA "did not exhibit signs of failure until 75" min and SBMA until 50 min; PEGMA failed at least 25 min earlier than SBMA and 50 min earlier than CBMA at low to intermediate cross-linker; CBMA stayed viable up to 30 min even above 60 wt% cross-linker; all hydrated films except 100 wt% cross-linker (PEGDMA) were unaffected by bending around cylinders of 2 to 32 mm diameter (p. 3130; methods p. 3126).
- n: 3 or more. Error bars SEM. Units: minutes.

### Figure 8 (p. 3130): friction after drying and rewetting (SBMA)

- Shows: coefficient of friction of SBMA hydrogels versus cross-linker percent, swollen (never dried) versus desiccated and rehydrated.
- Numbers, read from figure, approximate (absolute coefficient of friction): swollen about 0.03 up to about 50 percent cross-linker, about 0.04 at 67 percent, about 0.08 at 100 percent. Rehydrated about 0.03 at low cross-linker, about 0.06 near 33 percent, about 0.18 at 50 percent, about 0.22 at 67 percent, about 0.11 at 100 percent. Uncoated PDMS average 0.179 (caption, exact).
- Text exact: for under 25 wt% cross-linker the rehydrated and pristine coefficients are "basically identical"; above that the film flaked while drying and friction rose toward uncoated PDMS.
- n: 4 or more. Error bars SEM.

### Figure 9 (p. 3131): friction over 2500 cycles (about 17 hours)

- Shows: relative coefficient of friction (to uncoated PDMS, average 0.197), SBMA-coated, averaged over the first 40 min and the last 40 min (hours 16 to 16 h 40 min) of a 1000 min continuous test, versus cross-linker percent.
- Numbers, read from figure, approximate: about 0.06 for 5 to 13 percent cross-linker in both windows; about 0.10 at about 33 percent; about 0.2 at 50 percent; about 0.25 at 67 percent; about 0.25 (first) and about 0.33 (last) at 100 percent. A 0 percent cross-linker sample sits near 1.0 in the first window and about 0.7 in the last (this grafted polymer without cross-linker did not lubricate).
- Text exact: no statistical difference between the first and last 100 cycles. Test equals "nearly 17 h under force". Abstract says "up to 20 h". Flag: 2500 cycles is 1000 min or about 16.7 h in the main text; the abstract rounds up. Use "about 17 hours".
- n: 4 or more. Error bars SEM.

### Figure 10 (p. 3131): friction versus normal force (SBMA, 5 wt% cross-linker)

- Shows: coefficient of friction versus applied normal force 1 to 15 N.
- Numbers (read from figure, approximate, absolute coefficient of friction): 1 N about 0.029; about 3 N about 0.044; 5 N about 0.053; 10 N about 0.078; 15 N about 0.076. Uncoated PDMS at 1 N is 0.19 (caption, exact).
- Text exact: "a nearly linear increase" up to 10 N, "raising the coefficient of friction by 250%"; even at 15 N the coated value stays "below" and "less than half" that of uncoated PDMS. Wear tracks became visible at higher forces. Flag: reading 0.029 to 0.078 gives about a 170 percent rise, not 250 percent; confirm with the authors before quoting 250 percent.
- Context cited (ref. 71): maximum forces during CI implantation range between 0.18 and 0.42 N (p. 3131).
- n: 4 or more. Error bars SEM.

### Figure 12 (p. 3132): representative insertion force traces

- Shows: force (mN) versus time (ms) for one uncoated (thin line) and one SBMA-coated (thick line) electrode, per array type. A, Array Type 1. B, Array Type 2. Human cadaveric cochlea.
- Numbers, read from figure, approximate: Type 1 uncoated rises sharply from about 1000 ms to a maximum near 90 mN; Type 1 coated peaks around 25 mN, ending near 22 mN. Type 2 uncoated rises from the start to about 45 to 48 mN by about 2000 ms; Type 2 coated rises later to about 35 mN. Time axis runs 0 to about 2200 ms in this paper (Bennion 2021 shows 0 to about 5000 ms). Text exact: Type 1 uncoated max "around 90 mN", coated "only around 25 mN"; Type 2 uncoated "45 mN", coated "approximately 35 mN" (p. 3132).

### Figure 13 (p. 3132): group insertion force and work of insertion

- Shows: A, maximum insertion force (mN); B, work of insertion (microjoules), uncoated n = 9 versus coated n = 9, both array types pooled (Type 1 grey circles, Type 2 black squares).
- Numbers (read from figure, approximate): A, uncoated mean about 68 mN (spread about 45 to 92), coated mean about 35 mN (spread about 23 to 47), individual points from about 20 to about 110 mN. B, uncoated mean about 335 microjoules, coated mean about 235 microjoules (spread both about 200 to 300), individual points up to about 600 microjoules for uncoated. The paper prints no group means in the text for this figure.
- Text and caption exact: max force significantly reduced, ** p < 0.003; work of insertion tended to be reduced, p = 0.19 (not significant). Text says "a significant reduction (about 30%) in the maximum insertion force" and then "at least a 50% reduction in the maximum insertion force and a 30% reduction in the work of insertion". These two statements conflict; the figure means (about 68 to about 35 mN) support about 50 percent for max force and about 30 percent for work. Use the figure-based 50 percent only after confirming with the authors. Type 2 coated experienced about 20 mN less force across the whole insertion (p. 3132). Type 1 coated showed no difference for about the first half of insertion.
- Possible data overlap with Bennion 2021: the Figure 13 caption wording, n = 9 versus 9, two array types, the Slim Straight and Slim J arrays, and similar means (Bennion 64.2 versus 37.0 mN) suggest this may be the same experiment re-analysed (the work p-value differs: 0.05 in Bennion, 0.19 here; Fig. 12 time axes differ). Do not present the two papers as independent replications without confirming with the authors.
- Units: mN, microjoules.

Cautions for the site:

- This paper does not measure fibrosis in the cochlea, hearing, or trauma. Its statements about "less trauma and scarring" are inference.
- The paper's single foreign body response result is Figure 3 (capsule on flat PDMS, mice). The one-year implant evidence is in Horne 2023, which this paper only cites (p. 3127).
- No macrophage or cell counts are given.
