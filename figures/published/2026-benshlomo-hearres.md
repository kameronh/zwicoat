## Header

- **Citation:** Ben-Shlomo N, Peel A, Etler C, Mostaert B, Barrera G, Hansen KR, Hunger B, Fredericks D, Guymon CA, Hansen MR. Development of zwitterionic hydrogel thin film coatings of cochlear implant electrode arrays that reduce intracochlear inflammation and electrode impedance. Hearing Research 474 (2026) 109606. Received 22 Dec 2025, revised 20 Feb 2026, accepted 9 Mar 2026, available online 10 Mar 2026.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heares.2026.109606 (printed page 1).
- **Open access:** Yes, confirmed. Page 1 footer: "0378-5955/(c) 2026 The Authors. Published by Elsevier B.V. This is an open access article under the CC BY license (http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)." CC BY allows reuse, redistribution and adaptation, including commercial use, with attribution (credit the authors, link the license, indicate changes). Caveat: this is the license of the article as a whole. Anything that the article credits to a third party would not be covered; I saw no third-party figure in the PDF.
- **Competing interests printed (page 10):** C. Allan Guymon and Marlan Hansen are co-founders of ZwiCoat Materials Innovations LLC. Kameron R. Hansen (Dept. of Chemical and Biochemical Engineering) is an author, with roles listed as data curation, writing and validation.
- **Funding printed:** NIH R01DC012578 and T32DC000040.
- **Design:** pilot study, 3 female Suffolk-Dorsett sheep, each implanted in both ears (one coated, one uncoated, ear assigned at random), same surgeon, 4 weeks in vivo. Device: Advanced Bionics HiFocus SlimJ electrode arrays (human CI arrays). Coating: CBMA (carboxybetaine methacrylate) zwitterionic hydrogel, photografted and photopolymerized in one 10 min UV step in a custom 3D-printed mold.
- **One-sentence finding:** In this three-sheep pilot, CBMA-coated human cochlear implant arrays had about half the electrode impedance of uncoated arrays at implantation (total impedance and access resistance still lower at 4 weeks), and at the round window level had significantly fewer cells, fewer macrophages and less fibrous tissue in the scala tympani.
- **Authors' own caveats:** small pilot, "underpowered", wide animal-to-animal variability (sheep 1 had the strongest inflammatory response), variable insertion depth, sheep cochlea tapers faster than human so full insertion was not possible, 4 weeks only, no direct comparison to dexamethasone-eluting arrays, neoossification difference not significant.
- **Not in the PDF (in the online Supplement):** Supplemental Fig. 1 (electrode interaction matrix in PBS for SBMA-coated vs uncoated arrays; this is the transimpedance/channel interaction data), Supplemental Fig. 2 (neoossification volume per animal), Supplemental Fig. 3 (per-animal histology). The supplement is referenced as doi:10.1016/j.heares.2026.109606 (page 10). The lead would need to fetch it from the publisher site if channel interaction is wanted. Also note: the interaction matrix was measured on SBMA-coated arrays, not CBMA.

## Figures and tables

There are no numbered tables. Figures 1 to 6 are listed. Values marked "read from figure, approximate" were read by eye from the plotted bars on the PDF page (rendered at 200 dpi). Values marked "text" are stated in the article.

### Figure 1 (page 2): schematic

- Foreign body response on implants, how CBMA and PEGDMA photograft and polymerize, ultra-low fouling coating, sheep study for 4 weeks. No data.

### Figure 2 (page 3): custom mold and coating thickness

- **Shows:** (A) the two-O-ring mold filled with 40 microliters of prepolymer solution, stained with fluorescein; (B, C) uncoated and coated arrays dipped in fluorescein dye for 5 min (coating absorbs the dye) under light microscopy; (D) cross section of a coated array under confocal microscopy; (E) fluorescence and brightfield overlay.
- **Numbers, text:** hydrated coating "uniform 25 micrometer thickness" from proximal marker to distal tip (also page 4, page 6). Both PDMS carrier and platinum contacts coated. No delamination was seen on removal after 4 weeks (page 4).
- **n:** not stated for thickness. **Statistic:** none.
- **Units:** micrometers.

### Figure 3 (page 5): impedance components, coated vs uncoated, immediately and at 4 weeks

- **Shows:** (A) schematic of how a biphasic pulse gives polarization impedance (electrode-electrolyte interface) and access resistance (materials around the electrode, linked in the paper to fibrosis and neoossification); (B) total impedance; (C) polarization impedance; (D) access resistance.
- **Conditions:** Uncoated vs CBMA-coated; two time points: "Immediate" (intraoperative, right after array placement) and 4 weeks (just before harvest). Stimulus 64 microamp biphasic pulses, 161 microseconds per phase. Access resistance from voltage at 18 microseconds divided by current; total impedance from voltage at 161 microseconds; polarization = total minus access (Methods, page 3). Data from the 8 distal electrodes of each array (Discussion, page 7). All electrodes remained functional in both groups (page 4).
- **n per group:** The caption and text do not give an n. Each plotted dot appears to be one electrode. With 3 arrays per group and 8 electrodes per array, that would be up to 24 points per bar per time point. I have not confirmed this. Tell the authors if you use it; do not state an n on the site until they confirm. Note that electrodes on the same array are not independent, which matters for how strongly to word the p values.
- **Statistic (printed on the figure):** 2-way ANOVA with Sidak correction (Methods, page 4).
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate (kilo-ohms, bar heights):**

| Measure | Time | Uncoated | CBMA | p (printed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total impedance | Immediate | about 30 | about 10.5 | < 0.0001 |
| Total impedance | 4 weeks | about 17.5 | about 9.5 | 0.0007 |
| Polarization impedance | Immediate | about 20 | about 6 | < 0.0001 |
| Polarization impedance | 4 weeks | about 9.5 | about 6 | 0.2586 (not significant) |
| Access resistance | Immediate | about 12.5 | about 6 | 0.0019 |
| Access resistance | 4 weeks | about 11 | about 5 | 0.0084 |

  - Individual points: uncoated total impedance immediate ranges from about 15 to 51 kOhm; CBMA about 5 to 22 kOhm. Error bars are shown (SD assumed; the caption does not say what they are).
  - Derived from those approximate bars, CBMA is about 65% lower in total impedance immediately and about 45% lower at 4 weeks. The authors state "about 50% less than uncoated arrays" (page 9). Use the author statement, not my arithmetic.
- **Units:** kOhm (kiloohms).

### Figure 4 (page 6): X-ray microscopy (XRM) of neoossification

- **Shows:** (A, B) axial XRM slice of an uncoated cochlea (neoossification marked by arrow) and a CBMA-coated cochlea; (C, D) semi-transparent 3D reconstructions with the implant (purple) in place (the coated example has redundant array in the basal cochlea, marked with a star); (E, F) 3D segmentation of neoossification in red. Scale bar 2 mm.
- **Numbers, text (Section 3.3, page 4):** Neoossification volume was "over an order of magnitude" greater in uncoated arrays: mean 12.24 x 10^-3 mm3 (uncoated) vs 0.795 x 10^-3 mm3 (CBMA). Not statistically significant ("did not reach statistical significance", Supplemental Fig. 2). The authors say the study is underpowered for this, with low neoossification overall. Mostly at the first turn of the cochlea.
- **n:** 3 sheep per group (inferred; not printed for this measure). **Statistic:** not significant; no p printed in the main PDF.
- **Units:** mm3.
- **Placement:** XRM confirmed intracochlear (scala tympani) placement of at least 8 electrodes, covering at least 180 degrees of the first turn, with no translocation into scala media and no tip fold-over (page 4).

### Figure 5 (page 7): histology at the basal turn, mid-modiolar sections

- **Shows:** (A, B) H&E of uncoated and CBMA-coated cochleae (sheep 1), star marks inflammatory infiltrate in the scala tympani; (C, D) immunofluorescence: alpha-SMA fibrosis (red), Iba1 macrophages (green), Hoechst nuclei (blue). (E, F, G) quantification. Scale bars 500 micrometers.
- **Conditions:** per sheep (3 sheep) and "All" (pooled across animals). Each per-sheep bar is from 3 adjacent sections (Methods, page 4); dots in the "All" bar are the 3 sheep.
- **Statistic:** 2-way ANOVA with Sidak correction. p printed only where significant.
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate** (cell density and macrophage density are per 10^5 cubic micrometers; fibrosis is percent of scala tympani volume):

| Measure | Sheep 1 uncoated | Sheep 1 CBMA | Sheep 2 U / C | Sheep 3 U / C | All U / C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell nuclei density | about 48 | about 26 (p = 0.0329) | about 26 / 14 | about 12 / 17 | about 29 / 19 (ns) |
| Macrophage density | about 34 | about 15 (p = 0.0042) | about 12 / 8 | about 7 / 11.5 | about 17.5 / 11 (ns) |
| Fibrosis (alpha-SMA volume %) | about 60 | about 12 (p = 0.0002) | about 18 / 3 | about 6 / 5 | about 27 / 6 (ns) |

  - Only sheep 1 reached significance here; the pooled "All" comparison is "ns" in all three panels (text, page 9: "at the level of mid modiolar sections, the difference failed to reach statistical significance in this small pilot study"). Sheep 3 shows CBMA slightly higher or equal to uncoated in this location.
- **Axis label caveat:** the y axes read "Cell nuclei/um3 x10^5". The Methods say density "per 10^5 um3" (page 4), so a value of 48 means 48 per 10^5 cubic micrometers. I am taking the Methods reading.

### Figure 6 (page 8): histology at the base, round window level (the main histology result)

- **Shows:** (A, B) H&E of uncoated and CBMA cochlear base at the round window opening (star marks infiltrate in the scala tympani in the uncoated ear); (C, D) immunofluorescence with alpha-SMA (red), Iba1 (green), Hoechst (blue); (E, F, G) quantification per sheep and pooled. Scale bars 500 micrometers.
- **Conditions and statistic:** as Figure 5. 2-way ANOVA, Sidak correction. The paper says (page 5) a statistically significant decrease was seen in cell nuclei density, macrophage density and proportion of scala tympani volume occupied by fibrotic tissue.
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate** (same units as Figure 5):

| Measure | Sheep 1 U / C (p) | Sheep 2 U / C | Sheep 3 U / C (p) | All U / C (p) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell nuclei density | about 24.5 / 4.5 (< 0.0001) | about 8.5 / 8.5 (ns) | about 24 / 2 (< 0.0001) | about 19 / 4.5 (0.0020) |
| Macrophage density | about 13.3 / 1.2 (< 0.0001) | about 4.5 / 5.5 (ns) | about 11.8 / 0.9 (0.0004) | about 9.8 / 2.6 (0.0063) |
| Fibrosis (alpha-SMA volume %) | about 29 / 3.5 (< 0.0001) | about 5 / 2.5 (ns) | about 15 / 1 (0.0242) | about 16.5 / 2.5 (0.0101) |

  - Sheep 2 showed no coating benefit in cell or macrophage density here, and its overall inflammation was low in both ears. Show this honestly if the figure is rebuilt.
  - Derived from the approximate pooled bars: roughly 4x fewer nuclei, 4x fewer macrophages and 6x less fibrosis in the coated ears at the round window. The paper does not state these ratios; do not put them on the site unless the lead gets raw values.
- **n:** 3 sheep, 3 adjacent sections each (Methods, page 4). Statistics treat sections as units for the per-sheep p values. The pooled p values rest on only 3 animals per group.

## Numbers quoted in the text

Abstract and results:

- Page 1 (abstract): "significantly reduced total impedance, polarization impedance, and access resistance" at implantation; reductions in total impedance and access resistance maintained at 4 weeks; XRM confirms intracochlear placement without translocation or tip fold-over and "a trend to reduced neoossification"; "significantly decreased cellular infiltration, macrophage infiltration, and fibrotic tissue deposition".
- Page 1 (introduction): over 600,000 cochlear implants performed over the past 30 years (cited from other work).
- Page 3: coating 31.5 wt% CBMA, 3.5 wt% PEGDMA (average spacer molecular weight 400), 0.8 wt% surfactant, 0.05 wt% HEPK, 64.15 wt% water; benzophenone 50 g/L in acetone, 1 h, dried 20 min under vacuum; 40 microliters of prepolymer in the mold; 10 min UV at 30 mW/cm2 under nitrogen; fluorescein dye 1 g/L for 5 min to show the coating; 3 female Suffolk-Dorsett sheep; 4 weeks; stimulus 64 microamp, 161 microseconds per phase; access resistance read at 18 microseconds; at least 8 electrodes intracochlear.
- Page 4: 30 micrometer sections, 6 weeks of 0.2 M EDTA decalcification, 20x objective (0.70 NA), z spacing 1 micrometer, antibodies at 1:400; densities per 10^5 cubic micrometers; coating about 25 micrometers thick hydrated; 10 min one-step cure; no delamination after 4 weeks; electrodes at least 8 inserted, at least 180 degrees of the first turn; neoossification mean 12.24 x 10^-3 mm3 (uncoated) vs 0.795 x 10^-3 mm3 (CBMA), not significant.
- Page 4: all electrodes (coated and uncoated) remained functional; polarization impedance, access resistance and total impedance significantly lower in coated arrays at implantation; the text says "in saline and immediately after implantation" (the saline data are not in Figure 3 or the PDF supplement list); access resistance and total impedance still lower at 4 weeks.
- Page 7: polarization impedance of uncoated electrodes decreased after 4 weeks while coated stayed "largely unchanged"; 8 distal electrodes used for analysis.
- Page 8: human post-mortem CI literature: 10% to 30% of cochlear volume occupied by fibrosis or bone (cited from other work); neoossification in mice by 3 weeks (cited).
- Page 9: coating gives impedance reduction "about 50% less than uncoated arrays" (immediate and sustained), versus dexamethasone elution that only limits the later rise; permanent, covalent attachment; CBMA coating stable 1 year after subcutaneous implantation in mice (page 6, cited from prior work, Horne 2023).

## Candidate website charts

1. **"Half the electrode impedance, straight after surgery"** (strongest). Grouped bars with the individual electrode dots, three small panels. Series: total impedance, polarization impedance, access resistance (kOhm) for uncoated vs CBMA at Immediate and 4 weeks (Figure 3 B to D). Data: read from figure, approximate. The p values are printed and can be used exactly (< 0.0001, 0.0007, < 0.0001, 0.2586, 0.0019, 0.0084). Ask the authors for the per-electrode values to rebuild the chart exactly. Plain-language gloss: access resistance tracks tissue around the electrode; polarization tracks the electrode surface.
2. **"Fewer inflammatory cells, fewer macrophages, less fibrosis at the round window"** (strongest). Three small multiples, bars for each of 3 sheep plus the pooled result, uncoated vs CBMA. Series: nuclei density, macrophage density (per 10^5 cubic micrometers), alpha-SMA fibrous tissue (percent of scala tympani volume) (Figure 6 E to G). Data: read from figure, approximate; p values printed (exact). Show sheep 2 honestly.
3. **"Same pattern deeper in the cochlea, but weaker"** (strongest as an honest companion to chart 2). Fibrosis percent per sheep, round window (Figure 6G) beside the basal turn (Figure 5G): the effect is large in sheep 1 at both sites, but pooled basal-turn results are not significant. Data: read from figure, approximate. This is what a careful R&D lead will want to see; it also protects the site from over-claiming.
4. **"Access resistance, a clinical marker of fibrosis, stays lower at 4 weeks"** Dot plot or paired bars: access resistance (kOhm) uncoated vs CBMA, Immediate and 4 weeks (Figure 3D). Data: read from figure, approximate; p = 0.0019 and 0.0084 exact. It is a subset of chart 1 and could replace it for a simpler page.
5. **"New bone growth in the cochlea after 4 weeks"** Two bars: neoossification volume, uncoated 12.24 x 10^-3 mm3 vs CBMA 0.795 x 10^-3 mm3. Data: exact (text, page 4).
