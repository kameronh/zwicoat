## Important notes before using any patent number on the site

1. **Patent examples are not peer-reviewed publications.** The owner's rule is "only data from published papers may appear on the website." Patent specification data is published in the legal sense but is not a paper. Treat all numbers below as "patent only", and use them on the site only if the lead decides patents count, or use them only as pointers for what to request from the lab.
2. **Much of the cochlear in vivo section is written as a plan, not as results.** Columns 25 to 27 describe the rat or mouse cochlear implant experiment in conditional language ("may be", "may be implanted", Table 1 of 6 per group). The statements about "threefold reduction", "2 to 3 orders of magnitude", and preserved hearing (col. 30) are expectations and are not results. Do not quote them as data.
3. **Internal inconsistency on protein adsorption:** col. 22, lines 58 to 61 says CBMA showed the strongest fibrinogen reduction on glass (Fig. 4B); col. 30, lines 1 to 6 says on PDMS the SBMA surface adsorbed the least protein and CBMA second least (Fig. 23). My reading of the plotted bars agrees with each text for its own figure. Different substrates (glass vs PDMS), so this may be real, but it needs an explanation before a claim like "CBMA beats SBMA" is made.
4. **Likely typos in the printed specification:** col. 29 lists thickness embodiments "about 280 nm, about 2900 nm, about 300 nm" (the "2900" is probably 290); col. 22 says "Fibrinogen" experiments used "PEGA" in the Fig. 4 text but "PEGDA" elsewhere. The inventor name is printed as "Marian R. Hansen" on both patents (Marlan R. Hansen is the person on the paper authors lists; confirm before naming him).
5. The two patents have the same specification. The second is a division of the first. Everything under "Quantitative examples" applies to both unless noted. Column numbers are the same in both (checked on cols. 29 to 32).

## US 11,525,069 B2 (method)

- **Title:** Durable photopolymerizable cross-linked anti-fouling coatings
- **Issue date:** Dec. 13, 2022 (patent term adjustment 1017 days)
- **Assignee and applicant:** University of Iowa Research Foundation, Iowa City, IA (not ZwiCoat)
- **Inventors:** Elise Lin Cheng, C. Allan Guymon, "Marian R. Hansen", Braden Leigh
- **Application:** 16/312,448, from PCT/US2017/039153 (PCT filed Jun. 24, 2017; WO 2017/223544 published Dec. 28, 2017); provisional 62/354,527 filed Jun. 24, 2016; prior publication US 2020/0308440 A1 (Oct. 1, 2020)
- **Size:** 9 claims, 27 drawing sheets. Examiner Peter A. Salamon; attorneys McAndrews, Held and Malloy.
- **Abstract (paraphrase):** Durable, anti-fouling, cross-linked zwitterionic coatings are covalently grafted to a substrate. Under light, zwitterionic monomers react with a cross-linker and with radicals activated at the surface, which forms the coating and anchors it at the same time. Photomasks can micropattern the coating.

### Independent claim (col. 32)

- **Claim 1 (only independent claim):** A method for making a cross-linked anti-fouling coating on at least one surface of a medical-system component: first treat the surface with a substance that promotes coating adhesion, then apply a liquid containing a zwitterionic monomer, then polymerize the monomer with a light source in the presence of one of a listed set of cross-linkers.
- **Cross-linkers named in claim 1:** ethylene glycol dimethacrylate (EGDMA), poly(ethylene glycol) di(meth)acrylate (PEGD(M)A), propylene glycol diacrylate, bisphenol A ethoxylate diacrylate, N,N'-methylenebis acrylamide, pentaerythritol tetraacrylate, 1,3-butylene glycol dimethacrylate (BGDMA), 1,4-butanediol diacrylate (BDDA), 1,6-hexanediol diacrylate (HDDA), hexanediol dimethacrylate (HDDMA), neopentylglycol diacrylate (NPGDA), trimethylolpropane triacrylate (TMPTA).
- **Dependent claims in plain words:** 2: monomer contains a phosphoryl choline, sulfobetaine or carboxybetaine group. 3: monomer is carboxybetaine methacrylate (CBMA) or sulfobetaine methacrylate (SBMA). 4: light is ultraviolet. 5 and 6: pre-treatment is a hydrogen-abstraction photoinitiator, from camphorquinone, benzophenone, thioxanthone, 6,7-dichloro-5,8-quinolinedione, 1,4-naphthoquinone, naphthodioxinone-1,3-benzodioxole, derivatives, and mixtures. 7: the light activates the photoinitiator and polymerizes the monomer at the same time, bonding the coating covalently to the substrate. 8: a photomask blocks polymerization on part of the surface. 9: the monomer liquid also contains an added photoinitiator.
- **Substrates named in the claims:** "a surface of a component of a medical system" only; no specific substrate is named in these claims (the specification gives PDMS, glass, metals, below).
- **Monomers named in the claims:** carboxybetaine methacrylate, sulfobetaine methacrylate; moieties phosphoryl choline, sulfobetaine, carboxybetaine.

## US 11,814,526 B2 (system)

- **Issue date:** Nov. 14, 2023 (term adjustment 0 days)
- **Assignee and applicant:** University of Iowa Research Foundation

- **Size:** 16 claims, 27 drawing sheets.

### Independent claim (col. 31 line 52 to col. 32 line 8)

- **Claim 1 (only independent claim; claims 2 to 16 depend on it):** A system made of a substrate and a cross-linked hydrogel coating of a zwitterionic polymer (zwitterionic monomer units plus one of the listed cross-linkers), where the coating is covalently bonded to at least one surface of the substrate and is configured to resist adhesion by biomolecules, cells, tissue or bacteria.
- **Cross-linkers in claim 1:** same list as in the method patent, plus 1-carboxy-N-methyl-N-di(2-methacryloyloxy-ethyl) methanaminium inner salt (CBMX) and (meth)acrylamides.
- **Dependent claims in plain words (cols. 32 to 33):**
  - 2: the substrate is a component of a medical device.
  - 3 and 4: monomer has a phosphoryl choline, sulfobetaine or carboxybetaine group; named monomers are carboxybetaine (meth)acrylate, phosphoryl choline (meth)acrylate, sulfobetaine (meth)acrylate, carboxybetaine (meth)acrylamide, sulfobetaine (meth)acrylamide.
  - 5 and 6: substrate is metallic or a synthetic polymer: PDMS, polypropylene, polyurethanes, polyvinylidene fluoride, polyethylene terephthalate, polyethylene, polyketones, polystyrene, polyvinyl chloride, poly(meth)acrylates, poly(meth)acrylamides, polylactides, polyesters, polyether ether ketone (PEEK).
  - 7: the medical device is chosen from a long list: catheter, vascular stent, dental implant, contact lens, cochlear implant, surgical instrument, scope, probe, intravascular catheter, tubing (IV, arterial, central lines), ear tube, plate, screw, pin, rod, middle ear implant, artificial spine disc, endotracheal or tracheotomy tube, cerebrospinal fluid shunt, surgical drain, chest tube, endobronchial stent, urinary stent, bile stent, lacrimal stent, pacemaker, defibrillator, nerve stimulator, spinal cord stimulator, deep brain stimulator, hernia mesh, urogynecological mesh, intraocular lens, cosmetic implants, birth control device, drug delivery device, medical equipment, orthopedic implant.
  - 8: the orthopedic implant is a knee, hip, shoulder, elbow, wrist or ankle replacement or a component.
  - 9: the zwitterionic polymer is a copolymer.
  - 10: substrate is glass, glass ceramic or ceramic.
  - 11: coated and uncoated regions form a micropattern on the surface.
  - 12: coating thickness at least about 100 nm.
  - 13: monomer is CBMA or SBMA.
  - 14, 15, 16: polymer is cross-linked with a first photoinitiator and bonded to the surface with a second photoinitiator (14); the photoinitiators are activated together by a light source (15); the surface has a chosen pattern of coated and uncoated regions (16).
- **Substrates named in the claims:** PDMS and the other polymers listed in claim 6; metallic substrates (claim 5; the specification at col. 16 names stainless steel, cobalt, chromium, tantalum, magnesium, titanium, nickel, nitinol, platinum, iridium and alloys); glass, glass ceramics, ceramics (claim 10).
- **Monomers named in the claims:** as in claims 3, 4 and 13 above.

## Quantitative examples in the specification (shared by both patents)

Numbers read from the printed text unless marked "read from figure, approximate". Column and line values are the patent's own.

### Coating recipe and process numbers

- **Monomer concentration:** CBMA or SBMA at 0.1 to 20 wt% in PBS with 0.05 wt% HEPK photoinitiator (col. 18, bottom, and col. 21, lines 20 to 23).
- **Light dose:** 10 min at 16 mW/cm2 at 365 nm, mercury arc lamp, 2.54 x 2.54 cm photomask or slide (col. 19, lines 1 to 5).
- **Glass substrates:** 2.54 x 7.62 cm slides; silane coupling agent (3-(trimethoxysilyl)propyl methacrylate) (col. 18).
- **Contact angle:** silanized glass over 57 degrees (col. 21, lines 34 to 36); sessile drop, Rame-Hart Model 190 (col. 19, lines 6 to 10). The measured angles for the coated surfaces are not stated as numbers in the text.
- **Thickness embodiments (col. 29, lines 40 to 47):** at least about 100 nm; or about 150, 200, 250, 260, 270, 280, "2900" (printed), 300, 330 nm; or at least about 1 micrometer; or at least about 1 mm. No measured thickness of a real coated part is given. Compare the 25 micrometer coating on the CI array in the Ben-Shlomo paper.
- **Benzophenone application:** soaked from acetone; the Fig. 21 x axis spans about 0 to 1 g/L.

### Bond strength (adhesion)

- **Fig. 21 (col. 29, lines 48 to 59; drawing sheet 25):** adhesion of the coating to PDMS (maximum force per area, N/m2) rises with benzophenone concentration in acetone. Text is qualitative. Read from figure, approximate (g/L: N/m2): about 0.02 to 0.1 g/L: roughly 10,000 to 17,000; 0.5 g/L: about 44,000; 1.0 g/L: about 50,000. Error bars are large. Units and concentrations are as printed on the axes.
- **Fig. 22 (col. 29, lines 60 to 64; sheet 26):** shear adhesion of SBMA cross-linked films on stainless steel, activated with vinyl phosphonic acid (VA) vs unactivated. Read from figure, approximate (N/m2, "pre-swollen" case): VA-activated about 160,000; unactivated about 50,000. In the "swollen" case the values are far lower (VA-activated about 15,000, unactivated near 0). No n or p printed in the text.
- **Col. 29, lines 22 to 26:** states the shear strength of the bond is greater than the shear strength of the coating itself, so the coating "will not peel off under shear stress". No number.

### Protein adsorption

- **Fibrinogen on glass (col. 21, lines 40 to 61; Fig. 4B, sheet 4):** 1 mg/mL human fibrinogen, 1 h; fluorescence relative to uncoated glass; n = 3 slides, 10 images per slide. Text: CBMA, SBMA and PEGA all significantly lower than uncoated, CBMA strongest. Read from figure, approximate (relative to uncoated = 1.0): CBMA about 0.02; SBMA about 0.15; PEGA about 0.05.
- **Fig. 23, PDMS (col. 29 line 65 to col. 30 line 9; sheet 27):** relative protein adsorption on PDMS. Text: SBMA adsorbed least, CBMA second least, PEGDA more than either. Read from figure, approximate: uncoated 1.0, PEGDA about 0.12, SBMA about 0.01 to 0.02, CBMA about 0.05. The protein used is not named in the text of this paragraph. See note 3 above.

### Cells (in vitro, on glass)

- **Schwann cells, uniform coating (col. 24, lines 55 to 65):** SBMA gives "more than a 40 fold decrease" in cell density; CBMA "over 80 times" decrease. n and p not given in the text.
- **Schwann cells, striped coating (col. 24 line 65 to col. 25 line 11):** SBMA stripes 13-fold lower density; CBMA stripes "over 150 fold" lower; both p < 0.001 (t-test) vs adjacent uncoated bands.
- **Fibroblasts, uniform (col. 22, lines 28 to 32):** SBMA gives less than half the cell density of uncoated glass; CBMA less than 1% of uncoated.
- **Fibroblasts, striped (col. 22, lines 60 to 66):** zero fibroblasts on CBMA stripes.
- **Schwann cell alignment (col. 23, lines 53 to 60; Fig. 8C):** mean angle to the stripe direction: uncoated about 42 degrees, SBMA under 6 degrees, CBMA just under 2 degrees. Fig. 8C and 8D show n = 79, 90, 40 and n = 207, 236, 207 (read from figure, approximate).
- **Spiral ganglion neurite alignment ratio (col. 24, lines 7 to 12; Fig. 8D):** glass over 2.2, SBMA about 1.5, CBMA 1.09 (1 = perfect alignment).

### Bacteria and platelets (in vitro and in vivo)

- **Colonization assay (col. 27, lines 30 to 48):** S. aureus ATCC 25923, 1 to 3 x 10^5 CFU/mL, 24 to 26 h, 37 degrees C. The patent says "preferably at least a 1, 2, 3 or 4 log reduction" (a target, not a result).
- **In vitro (col. 27 line 53 to col. 28 line 44; Figs. 12 to 15):** CBMA and SBMA on PDMS "significantly reduced" S. aureus and S. epidermidis adhesion in wet, dry, 24 h and 48 h tests; n = 10 on the figures. Bar values are not in the text (the figures are rotated bar charts of bacteria per 20x field; I did not read them).
- **In vivo bacteria, subcutaneous (col. 28, lines 62 to 67; Fig. 17):** fewer than 250 GFP S. aureus on CBMA vs close to 1,000 on uncoated PDMS (n = 33 and 40 shown on the figure).
- **In vivo bacteria, subcutaneous colony counts (col. 29, lines 1 to 5; Fig. 18):** fewer than 100 on CBMA vs close to 700 on uncoated PDMS (n = 7 on the figure).
- **Live imaging (col. 28, lines 45 to 57; Fig. 16):** about 10^8/mL bacteria injected next to implants; day 0 signals equal (p > 0.05); afterward the CBMA implants gave less fluorescence than uncoated. Time points days 0, 3, 7, 14, 21; n = 6 on the figure.
- **Platelet adhesion (col. 29, lines 6 to 19; Fig. 19):** CBMA and SBMA coatings "significantly reduced" platelet adhesion on PDMS (rat blood, 60 min, 37 degrees C). Read from figure, approximate (platelets per 20x field): uncoated about 330, SBMA about 120, CBMA about 45.
- **Rat femoral vein implant (col. 29, lines 20 to 36; Fig. 20A and B):** a drawing, not data.
- **Fibroblasts with and without serum (col. 27, lines 20 to 25; Fig. 11):** SBMA reduces more with serum; CBMA reduces more without serum. No numbers in the text.

### In vivo cochlear (mostly a plan, see note 2)

- **Col. 25 line 52 to col. 26 line 22:** mouse scala tympani scar tissue at 22 weeks after PDMS implantation (Fig. 9, a drawing). Planned: 3 mm x 0.5 mm PDMS slips or 3 mm platinum-iridium wires of about 0.5 mm diameter, 4 groups of 6 animals (Table 1); ABR at 4, 8, 16, 32 kHz at 1 and 3 months. No results reported.
- **Col. 27, lines 1 to 12:** Fibrosis measured about 2 mm and about 5 mm from the round window; organ of Corti 1 to 5 grade scale (Fig. 10A to 10E). A grading scheme, not results.

## Quick summary for the lead

- Both patents are University of Iowa Research Foundation patents. The method claim is the pre-treat, apply, light-polymerize sequence with named cross-linkers. The system claim is a substrate with a covalently bonded cross-linked zwitterionic hydrogel, with a very long list of devices and substrates.
- The only strong patent-only numbers that a device-company reader would care about are the fibrinogen and PDMS protein adsorption reductions (relative to uncoated), the benzophenone dose and shear adhesion trends, and the in vivo S. aureus counts. All are small-n, approximate when read from the drawings, and should not be used without the lead's decision about the "published papers only" rule.
