- Plain-language finding: Adding a second zwitterionic network on top of a grafted first network makes the coating swell up to about three times less and about twice as strong in adhesion, while it keeps low friction (more than 80 percent lower than bare PDMS), cuts fibroblast adhesion by more than 99 percent in vitro, and gives about 75 percent thinner scar capsules after 6 weeks under the skin of rats.
- What "dual network" means: a first zwitterionic hydrogel is grafted onto the PDMS surface, then it is soaked in a second monomer solution and cured again, so two cross-linked networks interpenetrate in one film.
- Materials: SBMA (sulfobetaine methacrylate) at 35 wt% monomer in water, PEGDMA (400 g/mol) as cross-linker (percent of total monomer), Irgacure 2959 photoinitiator, benzophenone to graft onto medical grade PDMS. Cure: 30 mW/cm2 for 10 min under nitrogen, repeated for the second network. Cross-linker was varied from 1 to 20 percent in each network.
- Page numbering: PDF page n is printed page 16327 + n. All pages below are printed page numbers. PDF page 10 (p. 16337) and part of 11 (p. 16338) are references; skipped.
- Supporting Information (free, ACS, not read): Figure S1 (in situ NMR conversion), S2 (gel fraction), S3 (methylene blue staining, no adhesive failure), S4 (storage modulus over cycles), S5 (single-network hydrogels at 55 wt% monomer). The main PDF only was read.
- Scope cautions for the site:
  - Only ONE animal experiment: flat coated PDMS disks implanted under the skin of rats for 6 weeks. No cochlear implant, no electrode array and no human tissue were tested in this paper. The introduction mentions cochlear implants as motivation only.
  - No clinical claims. The paper claims fewer fibrotic cells and a thinner capsule, which are performance results on a material, not outcomes in patients.
  - The abstract and conclusion state "the coefficient of friction reduced by over 80%" and "cell adhesion reduced by more than 99%". Both are best-case groups, not every group (see tables).

### Figure 2 (p. 16332): equilibrium swelling, single versus dual network

- Shows: swelling percent (mass gained on swelling relative to dry mass) versus primary-network cross-linker percent, in water (A) and in PBS (B). Open circles are single networks. Coloured points are dual networks, one colour per secondary-network cross-linker (1, 2.5, 10, 20 percent).
- Conditions: primary cross-linker 1, 2, 2.5, 5, 10, 15, 20 percent; secondary cross-linker 1, 2.5, 10, 20 percent.
- Numbers (read from figure, approximate; swelling percent):
  - Water, single network: 1 percent primary about 168, 2 percent about 150, 2.5 percent about 143, 10 percent about 121, 15 percent about 122, 20 percent about 97.
  - Water, dual network, 1 percent primary: about 113 (1 percent secondary), about 122 (2.5 and 10 percent secondary), about 124 (20 percent secondary). So dual networks cut water swelling by roughly 25 to 33 percent at low primary cross-linker. Above about 10 percent primary the dual and single values overlap.
  - PBS, single network: 1 percent primary about 588, 2 percent about 458, 2.5 percent about 358, 5 percent about 250, 10 percent about 200, 15 percent about 185, 20 percent about 165.
  - PBS, dual network, 1 percent primary: about 378 (1 percent secondary), about 312 (2.5 percent), about 205 (10 percent), about 217 (20 percent). Largest drop: about 588 to about 205, which is about 2.9 fold (derived).
  - PBS, dual network, 2 percent primary: about 275 (1 and 2.5 percent secondary), about 200 (10 and 20 percent secondary), against about 458 single.
- Text exact: swelling falls by "at least 35%" when a secondary network is added at example low cross-linker (p. 16332); abstract: "up to threefold reductions in swelling for hydrogels with lower crosslink density" (p. 16328). Above 5 percent primary cross-linker the secondary network adds little (p. 16332). Swelling is higher in PBS than in water; the paper attributes this to the antipolyelectrolyte effect, where salt ions disrupt the attraction between the positive and negative groups (p. 16332).
- n: 4 per point. Error bars: one standard deviation (mostly hidden by the symbols). Statistic: two-way ANOVA; the primary by secondary interaction and each factor alone were significant, p < 0.0001 (caption).
- Units: percent.

### Figure 3 (p. 16332): compressive modulus (A) and cohesive strength (B)

- Shows: A, compressive modulus (MPa) of free-standing films versus primary cross-linker percent, single (open circles) and dual networks (secondary 1, 2.5, 10, 20 percent). B, ultimate cohesive strength (kPa) of grafted coatings versus secondary cross-linker percent, for 2.5 and 20 percent primary networks; the grey shaded region is the single-network control. All swollen to equilibrium in PBS.
- Panel A numbers (read from figure, approximate, MPa):

  - Dual, 20 percent primary: about 0.37 with a 2.5 percent secondary network (the highest value, about 1.6 times the single network), about 0.245 with 1 percent, about 0.20 with 10 percent.
  - Dual, low primary (1 to 2.5 percent): single network about 0.005 to 0.01; with a 10 percent secondary network about 0.06 to 0.09. This is the "approximately 10 times" rise in the text.
- Text exact: raising the single-network cross-linker from 2.5 to 20 percent gave "more than tenfold" higher modulus; at low primary cross-linker the modulus "increased approximately 10 times when a 10% cross-linker secondary network" was added (p. 16332).
- Panel B numbers (read from figure, approximate, kPa):
  - Single-network control (grey): 2.5 percent primary about 64; 20 percent primary about 77.
  - 2.5 percent primary, dual: about 92 (2.5 percent secondary), about 113 (5), about 117 (7.5, the peak), about 98 (10), about 82 (15), about 97 (20).
  - 20 percent primary, dual: about 90 (2.5), about 125 (5), about 87 (7.5), about 139 (10, the peak), about 91 (15), about 100 (20).
- Text exact: dual-network strength peaks at 7.5 percent secondary cross-linker "nearly double that measured for the single-network control" (low primary); for the high primary network the best is a 10 percent secondary network, "approximately 75% increase over the single-network baseline" (pp. 16332 to 16333). Conclusion: "doubled adhesive strength compared to single-network hydrogels" (p. 16336). No adhesive failure at the PDMS interface (the failure was inside the hydrogel); confirmed by methylene blue staining (Fig. S3).
- n: at least 4 per point in A, 5 per point in B. Error bars: one standard deviation. No p values given for Figure 3.
- Units: MPa (A), kPa (B).
