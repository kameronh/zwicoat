# Shen et al. 2021: zwitterionic coatings prevent S. aureus and S. epidermidis adhesion to PDMS

## Header

- **Citation:** Shen N, Cheng E, Whitley JW, Horne RR, Leigh B, Xu L, Jones BD, Guymon CA, Hansen MR. Photograftable Zwitterionic Coatings Prevent Staphylococcus aureus and Staphylococcus epidermidis Adhesion to PDMS Surfaces. ACS Applied Bio Materials 2021, 4(2), 1283-1293. Received September 9, 2020; accepted January 3, 2021; published January 22, 2021. University of Iowa.
- **DOI:** 10.1021/acsabm.0c01147 (https://doi.org/10.1021/acsabm.0c01147)
- **Open access:** No. Page 1 carries the plain "2021 American Chemical Society" copyright line with no open-access or Creative Commons marking. Figure reuse needs ACS permission and author consent.
- **Funding and conflicts:** NIH R01 DC012578, U54 TR001356, T32 DC000040 and a grant from the American Otological Society. Authors declare no competing financial interest.
- **One-sentence finding:** A cross-linked pCBMA coating photografted onto silicone (PDMS) cut adhesion of two common device-infection bacteria by roughly 10-fold or more in lab tests, and in rats it lowered bacterial load around the implant early on, left far fewer bacteria (and far fewer live bacteria) on the implant at day 21, and still resisted S. aureus after two weeks inside the animal.

Substrate: medical-grade PDMS (Bentec), 10 mm discs (in vitro) and 3 mm discs (implants), 0.040 in. thick. Coating bath: 50 wt % CBMA, SBMA or HEMA, 2.5 wt % PEGDA (Mn 575), 0.1 wt % HEPK, 47.4 wt % water; benzophenone 10 wt % in acetone, 1 h soak; UV 30 mW/cm2 at 365 nm for 10 min. Coating thickness "less than 25 micrometres" (SEM, Figure 2). pHEMA is the non-zwitterionic hydrogel control. Bacteria: GFP-expressing S. aureus and S. epidermidis at 10^8 CFU/mL.

## Figures and tables

There are no numbered tables. Figure 1 is a process schematic. Figure 2 is an SEM cross-section with no numbers. In all bar charts, colours are black = uncoated, red = pCBMA, blue = pSBMA, green = pHEMA. Counts are bacteria per high-power field (hpf; 200x magnification). Per-condition error bars are described as mean plus or minus SD in the Figure 3 caption; later captions do not say.

### Figure 3 (PDF p. 4, journal p. 1286). Bacterial adhesion, 1 h, wet (suspension) and droplet (spray) exposure
- **Shows:** GFP bacteria counted on each surface after a 1 h exposure and PBS washes. Wet = immersed in suspension. Droplet = sprayed (about 200 microlitres) and allowed to dry.
- **Conditions compared:** uncoated PDMS, pCBMA, pSBMA, pHEMA; S. aureus and S. epidermidis.
- **Numbers, exact from text:** uncoated PDMS wet: ">500 counts/hpf"; uncoated and pHEMA droplet: ">200 counts/hpf"; pSBMA vs uncoated in droplet for S. aureus: more than 200 counts/hpf fewer (p < 0.05); pHEMA not significantly different from uncoated except as noted below; pCBMA significantly lower for both bacteria in both conditions (p < 0.05); pSBMA significant for S. aureus (p < 0.05) but not S. epidermidis (p > 0.05).
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate (counts/hpf):**

| Surface | Wet S. aureus | Wet S. epidermidis | Droplet S. aureus | Droplet S. epidermidis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uncoated | 1560 | 890 | 510 | 240 |
| pCBMA | 40 | 60 | 80 | 30 |
| pSBMA | 150 | 550 | 170 | 155 |
| pHEMA | 590 | 1500 | 215 | 400 |

  Error bars read: uncoated wet S. aureus about plus or minus 300; most others plus or minus 20 to 220.
- **n per group:** n = 10 fields per condition (caption); each condition repeated three times.
- **Statistic:** one-way ANOVA with Tukey. Wet S. aureus F = 19.46, wet S. epidermidis F = 25.82, droplet S. aureus F = 9.284, droplet S. epidermidis F = 23 (df = 39 for all); each p < 0.0001.
- **Units:** counts per high-power field (200x).
- **Text vs figure conflicts:** (1) The text says that in droplet exposure pCBMA surfaces held more S. epidermidis than S. aureus, but the plotted values show the opposite (about 30 vs 80). (2) The text calls the pHEMA S. epidermidis increase over uncoated "slight", but the wet bar is about 1500 vs about 890 (roughly 1.7 times). Use the plotted values with this caveat, or ask the authors.

### Figure 4 (PDF p. 5, journal p. 1287). Bacteria after 24 h incubation, with and without serum pre-soak
- **Shows:** adherent GFP bacteria after 24 h in bacterial suspension (10^8 cells/mL); samples with or without 1 h pre-incubation in fetal bovine serum (FBS).
- **Numbers, exact from text:** pCBMA showed significantly fewer bacteria at 24 and 48 h (p < 0.05), with reductions of "approximately 200 counts/hpf or more" in every pCBMA group; pSBMA gave mixed results (S. aureus significant only without FBS at 24 h). The text prints this as "p > 0.05", which contradicts the caption (p < 0.0001); treat as a typo.
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate (counts/hpf):**

| Surface | S. aureus no FBS | S. aureus with FBS | S. epidermidis no FBS | S. epidermidis with FBS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uncoated | 1370 | 1620 | 960 | 780 |
| pCBMA | 350 | 580 | 290 | 220 |
| pSBMA | 590 | 1430 | 560 | 400 |
| pHEMA | 1700 | 1590 | 820 | 870 |
- **n:** n = 10 fields per condition; triplicate experiments.
- **Statistic (caption):** S. aureus pCBMA vs uncoated: without FBS F = 73.36, df = 39, p < 0.0001; with FBS t = 2.982, df = 18, p = 0.008. pSBMA without FBS t = 14.78, df = 9, p < 0.0001. S. epidermidis pCBMA without FBS t = 3.766, df = 18, p = 0.0014; with FBS t = 2.749, df = 18, p = 0.0132.
- **Note:** only pCBMA's FBS result on S. aureus (about 3-fold lower, 580 vs 1620) is weaker than the no-FBS result (about 4-fold lower, 350 vs 1370). pSBMA lost most of its effect with serum for S. aureus (1430 vs 1620).

### Figure 5 (PDF p. 6, journal p. 1288). Bacteria after 24 h plus a second inoculation and another 24 h (48 h total)
- **Shows:** as Figure 4, but surfaces re-inoculated after 24 h and incubated 24 h more.
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate (counts/hpf):**

| Surface | S. aureus no FBS | S. aureus with FBS | S. epidermidis no FBS | S. epidermidis with FBS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uncoated | 640 | 480 | 1430 | 3560 |
| pCBMA | 30 | 120 | 60 | 90 |
| pSBMA | 190 | 220 | 100 | 100 |
| pHEMA | 1850 | 2020 | 4480 | 4090 |
- **n:** n = 10 fields. **Statistic (caption):** S. aureus pCBMA: no FBS F = 74.52, df = 39, p < 0.0001; with FBS F = 83.99, p < 0.0001. S. epidermidis pSBMA and pCBMA vs uncoated: no FBS t = 5.526 and 5.238, df = 18, p < 0.0001; with FBS t = 13.51 and 13.41, df = 18, p < 0.0001.
- **Note:** pHEMA, the non-zwitterionic hydrogel control, ended up holding 2 to 4 times more bacteria than bare PDMS at 48 h. That is a useful negative control: a hydrogel alone does not prevent adhesion.

### Figure 6 (PDF p. 7, journal p. 1289). Live imaging of bacterial load around implants in rats
- **Shows:** (A to J) IVIS fluorescence images of rats at days 0, 3, 7, 14, 21 after S. aureus inoculation, implants either under the skin (subcutaneous, "Sub") or passing through the skin (transcutaneous, "Trans"), uncoated vs pCBMA. (K) relative radiant efficiency (RRE, a unitless fluorescence measure) in the region of interest.
- **Conditions compared:** Sub-uncoated, Sub-pCBMA, Trans-uncoated, Trans-pCBMA.
- **Numbers, exact from text:** days 3 and 7: RRE significantly lower around pCBMA than uncoated for both implant types (p < 0.05). Days 14 and 21: lower around transcutaneous pCBMA implants (p < 0.05); the subcutaneous difference was not significant (p > 0.05). All groups declined over time.
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate (RRE, unitless):**

| Day | Sub-uncoated | Sub-pCBMA | Trans-uncoated | Trans-pCBMA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1.85 | 1.95 | 1.7 | 1.85 |
| 3 | 1.75 | 0.95 | 1.6 | 0.95 |
| 7 | 1.62 | 1.08 | 1.4 | 0.95 |
| 14 | 1.35 | 0.95 | 1.5 | 0.6 |
| 21 | 1.0 | 0.68 | 1.0 | 0.5 |
- **n per group:** n = 6 animals per group (24 rats in four groups, Methods). **Statistic:** p < 0.05 as above; test not named for the time series.
- **Units:** relative radiant efficiency (unitless, background-subtracted and normalised fluorescence). Inoculum: 1 mL of 10^8 CFU/mL S. aureus injected around the implant at implantation.
- **Note:** a fluorescence-based bacterial load is an indirect measure; Figure 7D gives colony counts.

### Figure 7 (PDF p. 8, journal p. 1290). Bacteria on implants explanted at day 21
- **Shows:** (A, B) GFP images of explanted uncoated and pCBMA subcutaneous implants. (C) adherent S. aureus counts on the implant surface. (D) viable colonies recovered by sonication and agar plating.
- **Numbers, exact from text:** pCBMA implants had "reductions in bacterial adhesion of >500 counts/hpf". Many viable bacteria remained on uncoated implants at day 21.
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate:** (C) uncoated about 940 counts/hpf (error bar to about 1050), pCBMA about 155 (about 6-fold lower). (D) uncoated about 690 colonies (error bar to about 850), pCBMA about 35 (about 20-fold lower).
- **n:** (C) t = 7.011, df = 69 (about 71 fields); (D) n = 6 implants. **Statistic:** (C) p < 0.0001; (D) p = 0.0006. The statistic for C is a t test.
- **Units:** counts/hpf; colonies (colony forming units from a 1:40 dilution plated, 0.1 mL; the plotted value is "Colonies" as printed).

### Figure 8 (PDF p. 8, journal p. 1290). Coating after two weeks in the animal, then challenged with S. aureus in a dish
- **Shows:** subcutaneous implants (no bacteria at implantation) removed after 2 weeks, UV-sterilised, then incubated 24 h in 10^8 CFU/mL S. aureus.
- **Numbers, exact from text:** pCBMA implants had "reduction of >1000 counts/hpf" vs uncoated (p < 0.001), similar to non-implanted coatings.
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate:** uncoated about 2520 counts/hpf (error bar to about 2900), pCBMA about 210 (about 12-fold lower).
- **n:** n = 30 fields per group, t = 6.075, df = 58 (caption); 12 rats in two groups (6 per group) per Methods. **Statistic:** p < 0.0001 (caption) and p < 0.001 (text).
- **Note:** the 30 counts per group are fields of view, probably pooled from 6 animals (fields from the same animal are not independent). Report as 6 animals per group.

## Numbers quoted in the text

Page numbers are PDF pages (journal page in brackets).

- p. 1 [1283], abstract: significant reduction in bacterial adhesion and growth in vitro and in vivo, particularly for pCBMA; graphical abstract says coatings "decrease bacteria adhesion by an order of magnitude" (a claim in the graphic, supported roughly by the data: 6 to 40-fold for pCBMA).
- p. 1 [1283], intro: the "first 6 h after implantation" is the critical window for bacterial adhesion (cited background, not measured here).
- p. 2 [1284], methods: bacteria cultured to OD600 and diluted to 10^8 CFU/mL; BP 10 wt % soak 1 h; bath 50 wt % monomer, 2.5 wt % PEGDA, 0.1 wt % HEPK, 47.4 wt % water; UV 30 mW/cm2, 10 min; droplet test 200 microlitres sprayed, 1 h; wet test 1 h; 10 random fields at 200x.
- p. 3 [1285], methods: 24 rats (four groups), 300 to 350 g; implants 3 mm, 0.04 in. thick; 1 mL of 10^8 CFU/mL S. aureus injected; IVIS on days 0, 3, 7, 14, 21; explanted day 21; durability study 12 rats, 2 weeks.
- p. 3 [1285], results: thin films "less than 25 micrometres" thick (SEM).
- p. 4 to 5 [1286 to 1288]: wet-condition uncoated PDMS ">500 counts/hpf"; droplet uncoated and pHEMA ">200 counts/hpf"; pSBMA more than 200 counts/hpf fewer for S. aureus; pCBMA reductions "approximately 200 counts/hpf or more" in 24 and 48 h tests.
- p. 7 to 8 [1289 to 1290]: IVIS significance at days 3, 7 (both implant types), 14, 21 (transcutaneous only); implants at day 21: more than 500 counts/hpf fewer on pCBMA; after 2 weeks in vivo: more than 1000 counts/hpf fewer on pCBMA, p < 0.001.
- p. 8 [1290], discussion: bacterial load fell over time in all groups, but viable bacteria still remained on uncoated implants at day 21; largest difference between coated and uncoated was in the first few days.

## Candidate website charts

1. **"Bacteria on silicone in a lab test, bare vs coated (S. aureus and S. epidermidis)"**
   - Chart type: grouped bars on a log or broken axis, 4 surfaces (uncoated, pCBMA, pSBMA, pHEMA) x 2 bacteria, with the wet and droplet conditions as two panels.
   - Data series: Figure 3Q and 3R values above.
   - Data status: **read from figure, approximate**; the ">500" and ">200" counts for uncoated surfaces and "p < 0.0001" are **exact from text**. Directly relevant to infection-prone devices (catheters, leads, ear tubes).
2. **"Bacteria on implants taken out of rats after 21 days"**
   - Chart type: two paired bars (uncoated vs pCBMA), left panel counts per field, right panel live colonies recovered.
   - Data series: Figure 7C (about 940 vs 155 counts/hpf) and 7D (about 690 vs 35 colonies).
   - Data status: **read from figure**; ">500 counts/hpf fewer" and p-values **exact from text**. The in vivo result is the headline for a device R&D audience. Say "in rats, with a deliberately injected S. aureus load" in the caption.
3. **"Bacterial load around the implant over 21 days (rats)"**
   - Chart type: line chart with four lines (subcutaneous and transcutaneous, coated and uncoated), error bars, x = days after inoculation, y = relative radiant efficiency.
   - Data series: Figure 6K table above.
   - Data status: **read from figure, approximate**; significance by day is **exact from text**. Make clear the subcutaneous late difference was not significant (p > 0.05).
4. **"The coating still works after two weeks inside the body"**
   - Chart type: two paired bars (uncoated vs pCBMA, counts per field after a fresh S. aureus challenge).
   - Data series: Figure 8C (about 2520 vs 210 counts/hpf).
   - Data status: **read from figure**; ">1000 counts/hpf fewer" **exact from text**. Durability under real tissue exposure, but only 2 weeks.
5. **"Serum and repeated exposure: pCBMA holds, hydrogel alone does not"** (supporting)
   - Chart type: grouped bars from Figures 4 and 5 (24 h and 48 h, with and without serum).
   - Data series: tables above. Data status: **read from figure**. Shows pHEMA (a plain hydrogel) is worse than bare PDMS at 48 h while pCBMA stays low.

Caution for website use: bacterial results are for two Staphylococcus strains, a single rat model, 21 days, and an injected inoculum. The paper does not test biofilm maturation beyond 48 h in vitro, other organisms, antibiotic interaction, or any human outcome. Wording must stay at "reduced bacterial adhesion in the lab and in a rat model", not infection prevention in patients.
