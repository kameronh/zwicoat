- **Citation:** Horne R, Escudero C, Ellerman M, Guymon CA, Hansen M. Enhancing Mucus Flow and Clearance by Grafting Zwitterionic Hydrogel Films to Luminal Surfaces. Laryngoscope Investigative Otolaryngology 2025; 10(2): e70118. Received 15 Oct 2024, revised 6 Feb 2025, accepted 20 Feb 2025.

- **Conflict of interest printed:** C. Allan Guymon and Marlan Hansen are co-founders of ZwiCoat Materials Innovations LLC (page 7).
- **Funding printed:** NIDCD 1F30DC019274, 5R01DC012578-08; NIGMS 5T32GM007337.
- **Level of evidence printed in abstract:** "Level 2, randomized individual trial". This is an in vitro bench study, so this label looks odd. Do not repeat it on the site.
- **One-sentence finding:** Zwitterionic and conventional hydrogel films grafted onto silicone (PDMS) sheets and tubes lowered the tilt angle needed to start mucus flow, with the largest benefit on flat sheets and larger tubes, and a small, preliminary gain in how fast a dried mucus plug cleared.
- **Material:** PDMS (Bentec Medical sheets; tubes 4 mm long). Coatings: pSBMA (sulfobetaine methacrylate), pCBMA (carboxybetaine methacrylate), pPEGMA (poly(ethylene glycol) methacrylate, a non-zwitterionic hydrogel), made with PEGDMA cross-linker, photografted with benzophenone and UV.
- **Mucus model:** harvested from banana slug (Ariolimax columbianus) mucin vesicles, diluted to 5, 10 or 20% (v/v of concentrated vesicle extract in saline). The authors call it a model with fidelity to pig airway mucus. It is not human mucus. Plugging model adds 80 mg/mL pig gastric mucin.
- **Supplementary material:** The PDF refers to "Supporting Information" and Figure S1 (volume of mucus to start flow at a fixed 14 degree angle). Not in this PDF. Raw data "available upon request" (page 3).

## Figures and tables

Values marked "read from figure, approximate" were read by eye from the plotted bars or points on the PDF page. Values marked "text" are stated in words or numbers in the article. Where the figure reading and the text disagree I say so.

### Figure 1 (page 3): mucus contact angle on coated and uncoated PDMS

- **Shows:** Panels A to D are photos with the drawn angle of a 10 microliter mucus droplet on (A) uncoated PDMS, (B) pSBMA, (C) pCBMA, (D) pPEGMA, all at 10% cross-linker. The caption lists an (E) plot of contact angle vs mucus solution strength (0, 5, 10%); in the PDF the plot sits under panels A to D.
- **Conditions:** 4 surfaces x 3 mucus strengths (0% = saline, 5%, 10%). Hydrogels at 10% cross-linker.
- **Numbers, text (page 4, Section 3.1):** Uncoated PDMS contact angle "over 100 degrees" at all concentrations. At 0% mucus (saline), pSBMA and pCBMA give 0 degrees. pPEGMA gives 50 degrees at 0%. For 5% and 10% mucus, pCBMA and pSBMA are described as about 15 degrees (pCBMA) and about 30 degrees (pSBMA). pPEGMA is described as 30 degrees at 5% and 10% mucus.
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate (degrees; 0%, 5%, 10% mucus):**
  - Uncoated PDMS: about 107, 104, 98
  - pPEGMA: about 50, 37, 40
  - pCBMA: 0, about 13, about 13
  - pSBMA: 0, about 35, about 40
  - Note: the plotted pSBMA points (about 35 to 40) and pPEGMA points (about 37 to 40) look higher than the text values (30 for pSBMA, 30 for pPEGMA). Use the text values or ask the authors for raw data.
- **n:** 4 per mucus strength and sample type (caption). Error bars are standard deviation.
- **Statistic:** None printed for this figure.
- **Units:** degrees. Mucus strength is the volume percent of concentrated slug-mucin vesicle extract in saline.

### Figure 2 (page 4): tilt angle to start flow of a 60 microliter drop on flat sheets

- **Shows:** Panels A to C are schematics of the test (drop placed, plane tilted, drop flows to the far edge). Panel D plots angle to initiate flow vs mucus strength.
- **Conditions:** Uncoated PDMS, pPEGMA, pCBMA, pSBMA, all 10% cross-linker; mucus 0% (saline), 5%, 10%, 20%.
- **Numbers, text (pages 4 to 5, Section 3.2):**
  - Saline: flow occurs immediately even when flat (0 degrees) on all hydrogels; uncoated PDMS needs about 45 degrees.
  - 5% mucus: pPEGMA needs 5 degrees; uncoated PDMS needs 40 degrees. The text says a "similar pattern" for the other hydrogels (10 degrees or less).
  - 10% mucus: all hydrogels need "10 degrees or less"; uncoated PDMS needs 30 degrees.
  - 20% mucus: uncoated PDMS 35 degrees; pSBMA, pCBMA and pPEGMA need 20, 10 and 5 degrees respectively.
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate (degrees; 0%, 5%, 10%, 20%):**
  - Uncoated PDMS: about 44, 40, 30, 33
  - pPEGMA: about 0, 5, 4, 6
  - pCBMA: about 0, 0, 7, 11
  - pSBMA: about 0, 0, 9, 19
  - Several error bars are large (for example pPEGMA at 5%, up to about 13 degrees; pCBMA and pSBMA at 20%, up to about 25 degrees).
- **n:** 4 to 6 per mucus strength and sample type (caption). Error bars: standard deviation.
- **Statistic:** None printed on the figure. Methods (page 3) say Tukey tests were used for 3 or more groups.
- **Units:** degrees from horizontal.

### Figure 3 (page 4): effect of cross-link density on tilt angle to start flow (pSBMA)

- **Shows:** Tilt angle to start flow of a 60 microliter 5% mucus drop on pSBMA with different cross-linker percentages, plus uncoated PDMS for comparison.
- **Conditions:** Cross-linker percent 0, 5, 10, 20, 50, 75, 100 (points read from figure), and uncoated PDMS. 5% mucus (standard, Methods page 3).
- **Numbers, text (Section 3.3, page 4):** With no cross-linker, pSBMA behaves like uncoated PDMS (35 degrees). With cross-linking, the angle falls by "40% to 100%". Flow starts most easily (0 to 10 degrees) at 10% to 50% cross-linking. At the extremes (5%, and 75% to 100%) flow needs an intermediate angle of 10 to 20 degrees.
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate (cross-linker %, degrees):** 0%: about 33; 5%: about 13; 10%: about 0; 20%: about 4; 50%: about 7; 75%: about 17; 100%: about 22. Uncoated PDMS: about 35 (text says 35).
- **n:** 6 (caption). Error bars: standard deviation.
- **Statistic:** None printed.
- **Units:** degrees; cross-linker in percent of the polymerizable units (percent range 0 to 100).

### Figure 4 (page 5): tilt angle to start flow in large tubes, coated vs uncoated

- **Shows:** (A) photo of an uncoated tube (left) and a coated tube (right) dyed with orange fluorescein; (B) x4 microscope image of the uncoated tube wall; (C) x4 microscope image of the coated tube wall with the coating labeled; (D) bar graph of tilt angle for a 300 microliter bolus of mucus.
- **Conditions:** Uncoated PDMS tube, 2.6 mm inner diameter, vs pSBMA-coated tube of 1.6 mm functional inner diameter (the caption says "2.6 mm" and "1.6 mm functional"). Cross-linker 20%. Mucus is 5% (standard).
- **Numbers, text (Section 3.4, page 4):** Coated tubes needed 31 degrees vs 48 degrees for uncoated. The fixed-angle (14 degrees) volume experiment, in Figure S1 (not in the PDF), needed 700 microliters (coated) vs 950 microliters (uncoated).
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate:** Uncoated about 48 to 49 degrees; coated about 31 to 32 degrees.
- **n:** 6 per group (caption).
- **Statistic:** p = 0.0017 (Student's t-test), on the figure and in the caption.
- **Units:** degrees from horizontal.
- **Inconsistency to know about:** the Discussion (page 6) describes the coating as reducing the 2.6 mm tube "down to 1.5 mm", while the caption and Methods say 1.6 mm. The conclusions (page 7) say "about 2.5 mm". Use "about 1.6 mm" as in the figure caption and ask the authors.

### Figure 5 (page 5): tilt angle to start flow in small tubes (1.0 mm functional diameter)

- **Shows:** Bar graph with individual points. Cartoon tube cross-sections above the bars show the geometry.
- **Conditions:** (1) uncoated PDMS tube 1.6 mm inner diameter, (2) uncoated PDMS tube 1.0 mm inner diameter, (3) pSBMA-coated tube with 1.0 mm functional diameter, (4) pPEGMA-coated tube with 1.0 mm functional diameter (coating photografted in a 1.6 mm tube with a 1.0 mm spacer). Cross-linker 20%. Mucus 5%.
- **Numbers, text (Section 3.5, page 5):** Coated tubes need an angle of "just 35 degrees". The uncoated tube of the same (1.0 mm) diameter needs 46 degrees. The largest uncoated tube (1.6 mm) needs 44 degrees. The difference between coated and uncoated is 11 degrees (1.0 mm tube), 17 degrees (1.6 mm tube) and 40 degrees (flat sheet).
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate (degrees):** uncoated 1.6 mm about 43; uncoated 1.0 mm about 46; pSBMA about 35; pPEGMA about 34.
- **n:** 6 per group (caption).
- **Statistic:** One-way ANOVA p = 0.014; Tukey tests show coated vs uncoated 1.0 mm tubes differ: pSBMA p = 0.047, pPEGMA p = 0.036 (caption). The Discussion (page 6) states the 20% raw reduction vs the 1.6 mm uncoated tube is "not statistically significant".
- **Units:** degrees from horizontal.

### Figure 6 (page 6): mucus plug clearance time, Kaplan-Meier style

- **Shows:** Fraction of tubes that have "unplugged" (first fresh drops of saline discharge through the dried mucus plug) over time in saline.
- **Conditions:** Uncoated PDMS 1.6 mm ID, uncoated PDMS 1.0 mm ID, pPEGMA-coated (1.0 mm functional diameter), pSBMA-coated (1.0 mm functional diameter). Cross-linker 20%. Plug: 5% mucus plus 80 mg/mL pig gastric mucin, dried overnight at about 20 degrees tilt, 37 degrees C, 0% humidity (Methods, page 3).
- **Numbers, text (Section 3.6, page 5):** Median time to unplugging 30 min for both uncoated tube sizes and 22.5 min for both pSBMA and pPEGMA tubes. First unplugging event: 3 min in coated tubes vs 21 min in uncoated tubes (the text says "Three minutes required for the first unplugging event in uncoated tubes is also much less than the 21 min required for uncoated tubes", which is garbled; the figure shows the 3 min first event belongs to the coated groups). Statistic: log-rank (Mantel-Cox) p = 0.024 across groups.
- **Numbers, read from figure, approximate (minutes to fraction unplugged):**
  - pPEGMA: 0.33 at about 3 min; 0.5 at about 20 min; 0.67 at about 25 min; 1.0 at about 27 min
  - pSBMA: 0.17 at about 3 min; 0.5 at about 20 min; 0.67 at about 25 min; 1.0 at about 27 min
  - Uncoated 1.6 mm: 0.4 at about 21 min; 0.8 at about 30 min; 1.0 at about 31 min
  - Uncoated 1.0 mm: 0.33 at about 21 min; 0.67 at about 30 min; 1.0 at about 31 min
  - The fractions 0.4 and 0.8 do not fit n = 6, so my reading is uncertain; confirm with the authors.
- **n:** 6 tubes per cohort (caption: "n = 6 cohort").
- **Statistic:** log-rank p = 0.024.
- **Units:** minutes in saline; fraction unplugged (0 to 1).
- **Caution from the authors (page 7):** "modest, albeit statistically significant", an "inexact simulation" combining pig and banana slug mucus; they call it an early step.
