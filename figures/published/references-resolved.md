## What the PDF is, and what it is not (read this first)

1. The PDF holds TWO separate bibliographies, each numbered from 1: the ETT commercialization plan list (67 entries, "ETT-CP") and the ETT research strategy list (61 entries, "ETT-RS"). A bare `[ref n]` is ambiguous until you know which grant it came from. I checked the numbering against the text of `ZCMI_Research Plan_ETT_PLAINTEXT.pdf` and `ZCMI_ETT commercialization_PLAINTEXT.pdf` (for example RS 18-20 are the three subglottic stenosis papers, RS 23 is Chastre and Fagon, RS 42 and CP 67 are both Yin 2019). The match holds in every spot check.
2. The PDF does NOT cover the cochlear implant (CI-RS, CI-CP) or Phase I (P1) grants. Their reference numbers (for example "ref 14, CI-RS p1" for one million CI users, "ref 62, CI-CP" for hearing loss, "refs 73, 74", "ref 50", "refs 48, 49", "ref 78/79") are NOT resolvable from this PDF. Ask Kam for the CI Phase II bibliography.
3. Neither Li 2017 (World Neurosurgery) nor the spinal cord stimulator paper is in this PDF. Both came from the 2024 market memo (MR) as DOIs. I resolved them online (section 3).
4. Correction to the inventory: the ETT-RS "ref 56" for the "more than 99 percent" adhesion claim is Shen 2021 (RS 56), a published paper, not an internal source. But Shen 2021 reports 97 and 92 percent, not more than 99, so the inventory advice stands: use 97 and 92.
5. Correction to the inventory: "Murray et al., Lancet 2022, unconfirmed" is now confirmed. ETT-CP ref 39 is Murray 2022 and ETT-CP ref 40 is Jonas, World Bank 2017.

## 3. The six specific resolutions

Online checks used PubMed, Europe PMC, PMC and publisher abstracts, fetched 2026-11-02. Where I could only read an abstract, I say so.

### (a) Subglottic stenosis in 0.3 to 11.4 percent of intubations

- Where the grant got it: ETT-RS p1, refs 18-20 (Schweiger 2013, Dorris 2021, Manica 2013).
- The PDF does not settle it. Online check:
  - Dorris ER, Russell J, Murphy M. Post-intubation subglottic stenosis: aetiology at the cellular and molecular level. Eur Respir Rev. 2021;30(159):200218. doi:10.1183/16000617.0218-2020 (PMC9489001, open access). Its introduction says the incidence "ranges from 0.3% to >11%", citing Shah and Carlisle, Anaesthesia 2019, and Schweiger 2013. So the 0.3 is Dorris quoting Shah and Carlisle (not opened by me).
  - Schweiger C, Marostica PJ, Smith MM, Manica D, Carvalho PR, Kuhl G. Incidence of post-intubation subglottic stenosis in children: prospective study. J Laryngol Otol. 2013;127(4):399-403. doi:10.1017/S002221511300025X. Abstract: 123 children, "The incidence of subglottic stenosis was 11.38 per cent (95 per cent confidence interval, 6.63-17.94 per cent)". This is the source of 11.4.
  - Manica 2013 (Laryngoscope 123(4):1049-1054, doi:10.1002/lary.23771): 16 of 142 children under 5, 11.3 percent.
- Verdict: the range is real but it mixes a review's wide range (0.3 to more than 11 percent) with one pediatric ICU cohort (about 11 percent). Safe wording is "from 0.3 percent to more than 11 percent, depending on the population" (Dorris 2021), or "about 11 percent of children under 5 intubated in intensive care" (Schweiger 2013).

### (b) 1 to 11 percent in neonates

- Where the grant got it: ETT-RS p2 cites RS 18 (Schweiger) and RS 41 (Choi and Zalzal). ETT-CP p2 cites CP 21 (Schweiger) and CP 22 (Stoler 2025).
- NOT SUPPORTED as a neonatal range. What the cited papers actually say:
  - Schweiger 2013: children under 5 in a pediatric ICU, not neonates. 11.38 percent.
  - Choi SS, Zalzal GH. Changing trends in neonatal subglottic stenosis. Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg. 2000;122(1):61-63. doi:10.1016/S0194-5998(00)70145-8. Abstract: 1 of 416 NICU infants (0.24 percent) needed surgery for stenosis; 0.49 percent of those intubated 48 hours or longer. The same hospital ten years earlier: 0.65 percent of all infants, 1.5 percent of those intubated 48 hours or longer.
  - Stoler NA, Segada NW, Varghese V, Gowda SH, Mehta DK. Consequences of NICU intubations: incidence, identifications, and interventions. Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg. 2025;172(2):635-641. doi:10.1002/ohn.1004. Abstract: 441 intubated NICU patients, 94 (21 percent) had direct laryngoscopy and bronchoscopy, "SGS overall incidence was 2.95% with balloon dilation in 6.4%". Abstract only (Wiley blocked the full text).
  - Walner DL, Loewen MS, Kimura RE. Neonatal subglottic stenosis: incidence and trends. Laryngoscope. 2001;111(1):48-51. doi:10.1097/00005537-200101000-00009. Abstract: older estimates 0.9 to 8.3 percent of intubated neonates; studies after 1983 below 4.0 percent; after 1990 below 0.63 percent; the authors put the current incidence "likely between 0.0% and 2.0%".
- Verdict: do not print "1 to 11 percent in neonates". Use "about 3 percent of intubated NICU infants in a 2025 cohort" (Stoler) if the page needs a neonatal number. The 80,000 neonates a year (RS 39, 40) is also unchecked.

### (c) About $5,287 per patient-year for subglottic stenosis

- Yin LX, Padula WV, Gadkaree S, Motz K, Rahman S, Predmore Z, Gelbard A, Hillel AT. Health care costs and cost-effectiveness in laryngotracheal stenosis. Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg. 2019;160(4):679-686 (online 2018-11-27). doi:10.1177/0194599818815068. PMC6443425.
- Confirmed, with a narrower meaning than the grant implies. Abstract: "patients with intubation-related stenosis had significantly higher annual costs ($5286.56 vs $2873.62, P = .03)". That is intubation-related stenosis (17 patients) against idiopathic stenosis (17 patients). Annual costs are in 2017 US dollars, computed as total health care costs over the 4-year study period divided by each patient's time in care. The overall mean for related visits was $4,080.09 per patient per year.
- Wording: say "intubation-related stenosis", say "in a 34-patient study", and do not say "lifelong" on the strength of this paper.

### (d) Li 2017, World Neurosurgery, 36.6 percent of 8,275 cranial reconstructions

- Li A, Azad TD, Veeravagu A, Bhatti I, Long C, Ratliff JK, Li G. Cranioplasty complications and costs: a national population-level analysis using the MarketScan Longitudinal Database. World Neurosurg. 2017;102:209-220. doi:10.1016/j.wneu.2017.03.022 (PMID 28315803, online 2017-03-16).
- Confirmed from the abstract: patients "undergoing cranioplasty between 2007 and 2014 in the MarketScan national database", n = 8,275 (45.2 percent male, mean age 44.0), "Overall complication rate was 36.6%, mortality rate was 0.5%, and 30-day readmission rate was 12.0%".
- Two cautions for the site text:
  1. The abstract gives no follow-up period and does not define "complication". The site's "36.6 percent had a complication within seven years" (peek-and-orthopedic.md) is not supported by the abstract; 2007 to 2014 is the window in which patients were identified. Drop "within seven years" unless Kam checks the full text (Elsevier blocked me).
  2. The title says "cranioplasty", not "cranial reconstruction". The cohort includes autograft and synthetic implants, so the figure is not specific to PEEK (the page already says so).

### (e) Spinal cord stimulator removal, DOI 10.1097/PTS.0000000000000971

- Jones CMP, Abdel Shaheed C, Ferreira G, Mannix L, Harris IA, Buchbinder R, Maher CG. Spinal cord stimulators: an analysis of the adverse events reported to the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration. J Patient Saf. 2022;18(5):507-511 (online 2022-01-24). doi:10.1097/PTS.0000000000000971. Free full text PMC9329040.
- The abstract sentence: "The ratio of removals to implants was 4 per every 10 implanted." That is all the abstract says about removals. Per the article's methods, the implant and removal counts come from Australian hospital data (the Admitted Patient Care Minimum Data Set), not from the adverse event reports. The adverse event analysis covers 520 reports to the Therapeutic Goods Administration, July 2012 to January 2019: most rated severe (79 percent) or life-threatening (13 percent), device malfunction the most common (56.5 percent), surgical intervention the most common response (80 percent).
- Cautions: (1) the abstract does not say the removals were "for complications" or "including fibrosis". Both the inventory ("removed for complications including fibrosis") and `neural-interfaces.md` ("removed for complications in roughly four in ten cases") overstate it. Say "removals", not "removals for complications", and do not tie it to fibrosis. (2) The article is about adverse events, not fibrosis. (3) I did not retrieve the years covered by the removal counts.
- The inventory's other DOI, 10.1016/j.wneu.2017.05.152, is Al Tamimi M, Aoun SG, Gluf W. Spinal cord compression secondary to epidural fibrosis associated with percutaneously placed spinal cord stimulation electrodes: case report and review of the literature. World Neurosurg. 2017;104:1051.e1-1051.e5. A single case report with a literature review. Its abstract calls the complication "uncommon" and does not mention a "tolerance phenomenon". Do not cite it for that.

### (f) VAP cost ($40,000 to $44,000) and VAP incidence (8 to 28 percent)

- What `airway.md` already cites: Zimlichman 2013 (for about $40,000, no DOI in its sources line) and Gil-Perotin 2012. It does not cite any source for 8 to 28 percent (it does not print that number).
- Incidence, confirmed. Chastre J, Fagon JY. Ventilator-associated pneumonia. Am J Respir Crit Care Med. 2002;165(7):867-903. doi:10.1164/ajrccm.165.7.2105078 (PMID 11934711). The abstract says VAP "continues to complicate the course of 8 to 28% of patients receiving mechanical ventilation". Note Papazian 2020 gives a wider "about 5% to 40%, depending on setting and diagnostic criteria", so 8 to 28 is a 2002 figure.
- Cost, confirmed for $40,144 only. Zimlichman E, Henderson D, Tamir O, Franz C, Song P, Yamin CK, Keohane C, Denham CR, Bates DW. Health care-associated infections: a meta-analysis of costs and financial impact on the US health care system. JAMA Intern Med. 2013;173(22):2039-2046. doi:10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.9763 (PMID 23999949). VAP cost per case: $40,144 (95% CI $36,286 to $44,220), 2012 US dollars. The $44,000 in ETT-CP and the $40,000 to $44,000 in ETT-RS cite Papazian and Zimlichman together, but Papazian's abstract has no cost, so $44,000 appears to be the top of Zimlichman's confidence interval, not a separate finding.
