# Bennion et al. 2021, Otology and Neurotology (insertion force of coated cochlear implants)

## Header

- Citation: Bennion DM, Horne R, Peel A, Reineke P, Henslee A, Kaufmann C, Guymon CA, Hansen MR. Zwitterionic Photografted Coatings of Cochlear Implant Biomaterials Reduce Friction and Insertion Forces. Otol Neurotol 42(10):1476-1483, 2021. (The brief gave page 1433; the PDF prints 1476 to 1483. Use 1476.)
- DOI: 10.1097/MAO.0000000000003288
- Open access: NO. The only licence line printed is "Copyright 2021 Otology & Neurotology, Inc. Unauthorized reproduction of this article is prohibited." No CC licence. Any reuse of figures or text needs publisher permission. Plotted numbers rebuilt as our own charts and cited are facts, but the figures themselves are not reusable.
- Coating chemistry: SBMA (sulfobetaine methacrylate), photografted onto PDMS (silicone) with a PEGDMA cross-linker. Arrays from two manufacturers (Cochlear Slim Straight, Advanced Bionics Slim J).
- Disclosures printed: Bennion has an American Neurotology Society research grant; Hansen and Kaufmann are co-founders of iotaMotion; Hansen has NIH grants.
- Plain-language finding: A thin zwitterionic coating cut friction on silicone by more than 90 percent and cut the peak force needed to push a cochlear implant electrode into human cadaver cochleae by about 40 percent.
- Page numbering: PDF page n is printed page 1475 + n. All page numbers below are printed page numbers.

## Figures and tables

There are no numbered tables. Figures 1 to 4 carry numbers. Figure 2 is images only.

### Figure 1 (p. 1478): friction of coated versus uncoated PDMS

- Shows: Panel A is a diagram of the pin-on-disk tribometer. Panel B is the coefficient of friction, normalised to uncoated, for coated and uncoated PDMS against three probes.
- Conditions: uncoated PDMS versus SBMA-coated PDMS. Probes: steel ball, ceramic ball, and a steel ball covered with guinea pig dermis.
- Numbers: Every uncoated group is set to 1.0 by definition. Coated groups, read from figure, approximate: steel about 0.06, ceramic about 0.06, dermis about 0.06 (each with a very small error bar). Uncoated error bars (SD, read from figure, approximate): steel up to about 1.14 (so SD about 0.14), ceramic up to about 1.37 (SD about 0.37), dermis up to about 1.25 (SD about 0.25). Text exact value: "greater than 90% reduction" for all three (p. 1478). Absolute coefficients of friction are NOT given in this paper (data are normalised).
- n: 4 per group for the dermis experiment (guinea pig dermal tissue, n = 4, p. 1477). n for the steel and ceramic groups is not stated.
- Statistic: Mann-Whitney two-tailed (stated for the insertion data in Statistics; assumed same for friction, not explicitly said). p < 0.0001 versus uncoated for every probe (three asterisks).
- Units: dimensionless, normalised to the uncoated mean.
- Test conditions (p. 1477): Anton Paar pin-on-disk tribometer, PBS immersion, 1 N force, circular wear pattern, 200 s.

### Figure 2 (p. 1479): characterisation of coated electrode arrays (images, no quantities)

- Shows: A and B, fluorescein-dyed coated versus uncoated arrays (two array types). C to F: coated array by SEM and EDS (sulfur signal present, silicon signal absent over coated region). G to J: uncoated array by SEM and EDS (no sulfur, strong silicon signal).
- Numbers: none. Scale bar 600 micrometres in C and G.
- Note: coating is described as slightly cracked in the SEM image (C), attributed to the drying needed for SEM. Cracking was not seen under fluorescein imaging (data not shown).

### Figure 3 (p. 1480): representative insertion force traces

- Shows: force (mN) versus time (ms) for one uncoated and one coated electrode of each array type during insertion into human cadaveric cochlea. A: uncoated Type 1. B: coated Type 1. C: uncoated Type 2. D: coated Type 2.
- Conditions: motorised insertion at 0.5 mm/s through the round window. Grey lines are uncoated, black lines are coated.
- Numbers (read from figure, approximate; single representative traces, not group means):
  - Type 1 uncoated: force near 0 until about 2000 ms, then rises to roughly 75 to 80 mN by about 4200 ms with large spikes.
  - Type 1 coated: rises more gently, peaking at roughly 45 mN near 3800 ms, ending near 35 mN at about 4200 ms.
  - Type 2 uncoated: rises from about 2500 ms, with spikes reaching roughly 95 mN near 4800 ms.
  - Type 2 coated: peaks at roughly 60 mN near 4400 ms.
  - Time axis runs 0 to 5000 ms. Force axis 0 to 100 mN.
- n: 1 trace per panel (representative). The underlying group is n = 9 uncoated and n = 9 coated (Figure 4).
- Statistic: none for the traces. The raw time series are not tabulated; they would have to be requested from the authors.

### Figure 4 (p. 1481): maximum force, force variation, work of insertion

- Shows: individual points with mean and spread, uncoated versus coated, for three metrics. Each point is one insertion, coloured or shaped by array type (Type 1 grey circles, Type 2 black squares).
- Conditions: uncoated (n = 9) versus coated (n = 9) human cadaveric cochlea insertions, two array types pooled. Six temporal bones, three insertions per cochlea (p. 1477 to 1478). One cochlea with damaged coating was excluded and replaced.
- Numbers (exact, from text pp. 1479 to 1480; mean plus or minus SD, with 95 percent CI):
  - A. Maximum force (highest 1.5 s average): coated 37.0 plus or minus 11.3 mN (95% CI 28.3 to 45.6); uncoated 64.2 plus or minus 17.9 mN (95% CI 50.4 to 78.0). p = 0.001 in text; caption says ** p < 0.002; abstract says p < 0.001. Report as p about 0.001 and flag the inconsistency.
  - B. Force variation (derivative of force over time, mN/s): coated 52.1 plus or minus 21.0 (95% CI 32.0 to 64.2); uncoated 89.9 plus or minus 42.9 (95% CI 56.9 to 123.0). p = 0.03 in text; caption says * p < 0.04.
  - C. Work of insertion (area under force curve, microjoules): coated 216.3 plus or minus 70.0 (95% CI 162.5 to 270.1); uncoated 285.0 plus or minus 57.32 (95% CI 240.9 to 329.1). p = 0.05 (borderline, marked # in the figure).
- Individual points (read from figure, approximate): max force uncoated about 46 to 100 mN, coated about 19 to 54 mN; force variation uncoated about 45 to 165 mN/s, coated about 20 to 72 mN/s; work uncoated about 215 to 375 microjoules, coated about 105 to 360 microjoules. Which points belong to which array type can be seen in the figure, but the per-array-type n is not stated in the text.
- Statistic: two-tailed nonparametric Mann-Whitney test (Statistics, p. 1478). Data pooled across both manufacturers.
- Units: mN, mN/s, microjoules.
- Derived by us from the text means, not stated in the paper: max force down 42 percent; force variation down 42 percent; work of insertion down 24 percent. The abstract rounds the max force reduction to "about 40%".

Statements to avoid on the website (hearing outcomes are hypotheses here, not results): the discussion says reduced insertion force "may translate into less insertional trauma" and "improved outcomes". The study did not measure trauma, hearing or fibrosis. The paper itself says in vivo confirmation is still needed.

## Candidate website charts

1. **Peak insertion force drops by about 40 percent with the coating.**
   - Type: dot plot with mean and SD (or paired bars with individual points), two groups.
   - Series: uncoated n = 9 and coated n = 9, maximum force in mN. Means and SDs are exact (64.2 plus or minus 17.9 and 37.0 plus or minus 11.3). Individual points are read from Figure 4A (approximate), so prefer showing only mean plus or minus SD and the 95% CI from the text, which are exact. p = 0.001.
   - Data quality: exact for summary statistics; individual points would be read from a figure.
2. **Force during insertion: coated arrays stay lower the whole way in.**
   - Type: line chart, two lines per array type (force in mN versus time in ms).
   - Series: the four representative traces of Figure 3, redrawn.
   - Data quality: read from figure, approximate only, and single examples. A redraw would need the raw traces from the authors. Alternatively use the newer Peel 2024 Figure 12, which has the same issue.
3. **Friction on silicone falls by more than 90 percent against steel, ceramic and tissue.**
   - Type: bar chart, uncoated = 1.0 versus coated for three probes.
   - Series: normalised friction coefficient, coated about 0.06 (read from figure) for steel, ceramic, dermis; uncoated fixed at 1.0. Text states "more than 90%" exactly.
   - Data quality: the ">90 percent" claim is exact (text); the exact bar values are read from the figure. Safer to plot only the stated "greater than 90 percent reduction" as a callout number, or request normalised means from the authors.
4. **Force variation (smoothness) falls by about 42 percent.**
   - Type: dot plot, same style as chart 1.
   - Series: uncoated 89.9 plus or minus 42.9 mN/s versus coated 52.1 plus or minus 21.0 mN/s, n = 9 each, p = 0.03.
   - Data quality: exact (text).
5. **Work of insertion: a smaller, borderline reduction.** Only include if you want a complete set. 285.0 versus 216.3 microjoules, p = 0.05. Exact (text). Honest framing: borderline.

## Images worth requesting

Not open access, so permission from Wolters Kluwer (Otology and Neurotology) is needed for any figure, or request original images from the authors (Hansen lab, University of Iowa).

- Figure 2A and 2B: side-by-side photographs of fluorescein-stained coated versus uncoated electrode arrays, two array types. Best single image from this paper for showing selective coating on a real array.
- Figure 2C to 2J: SEM and EDS maps showing sulfur (coating) present on coated array and silicon only on uncoated. Strong for a "coating covers the whole array" panel.
- Figure 1A: tribometer diagram (drawn illustration, not a photograph; the site rules prefer real imagery).
- No histology or insertion photographs in this paper.
