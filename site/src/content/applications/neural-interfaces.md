## The problem

A neural electrode works or fails at one place, the interface between the device and tissue. Within seconds of implantation, proteins coat the array. Over weeks, scar tissue walls it off. That scar sits in the electrical path. It can raise impedance, so stimulation needs more voltage and power. It can spread current, so channels lose selectivity. Better geometry, softer substrates and steroid elution help at the margins. None of them changes the surface where the response starts.

## What ZwiGuard does here

Every result below comes from a published study. No coated device has been implanted in a person.

**Impedance, in sheep.** The chart above shows it. Three sheep each received one coated and one uncoated human cochlear implant array. Total impedance was about 30 against 10.5 kilohms at implantation (p < 0.0001) and about 17.5 against 9.5 at four weeks (p = 0.0007), about 50 percent lower. Access resistance was also lower at four weeks (p = 0.0084). Polarization impedance was not significantly different then (p = 0.26). (Ben-Shlomo 2026, Hearing Research.)

**Inflammation, in sheep.** At the round window, the pooled coated ears had fewer cell nuclei (p = 0.0020), fewer macrophages (p = 0.0063) and less fibrous tissue (p = 0.0101). One of the three sheep showed no difference in cells or macrophages. At the basal turn the pooled differences were not significant. The authors call the study a small pilot. (Ben-Shlomo 2026.)

**Scar capsule, in mice.** Human cochlear implant arrays implanted under the skin for one year grew a capsule about 60 percent thinner when coated, 47 against 18 micrometres (p = 0.026). The thin capsule held over platinum contacts as well as silicone. (Horne 2023, Acta Biomaterialia.)

{{figure:W27}}

**Insertion force, in human cadaveric cochleae.** Coated arrays needed about 40 percent less peak force, 64.2 against 37.0 millinewtons (p = 0.001, nine insertions per group). Force variation fell about 42 percent. (Bennion 2021, Otology and Neurotology.)

## Why it drops in

One UV step grafts the hydrogel to the surface and crosslinks it. The sheep arrays were coated in a printed mold, giving a film about 25 micrometres thick when hydrated, over the silicone carrier and the platinum contacts. On silicone, adhesion tests show the film failing inside the gel before the interface lets go (Leigh 2019). Bonding to your lead material is measured in the feasibility round.
