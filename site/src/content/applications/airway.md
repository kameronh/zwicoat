## The problem

Stenosis comes first. A rigid tube scrapes the larynx on the way in, and the airway reacts to it for as long as the tube stays. In a review of nine studies, 83 percent of intubated ICU adults had laryngeal injury (Brodsky 2018). In children under 5 in intensive care, each extra five days of intubation raised the risk of subglottic stenosis about 50 percent (Manica 2013).

Pneumonia comes second. Biofilm was found on 95 percent of endotracheal tubes in one prospective ICU study (Gil-Perotin 2012), and each ventilator-associated pneumonia costs about $40,000 (Zimlichman 2013).

Antibacterial tubes address infection only, and only modestly. Silver-coated tubes lowered ventilator-associated pneumonia in a Cochrane review (risk ratio 0.64), and metal-coated tubes did the same in a meta-analysis of five trials (0.71), with no change in hospital mortality (Tokmaji 2015; Yang 2024). The forest plot above shows the trials. Neither does anything for friction or the foreign body response, and neither stops mucus from plugging a tracheostomy tube.

## What ZwiCoat does here

The results below come from published studies on silicone and on cochlear implant arrays, the models for mucus flow, friction and insertion force.

**Mucus flow, in silicone tubes.** In a 2.6 millimeter silicone tube, mucus started to flow at 31 degrees of tilt against 48 uncoated. Dried mucus plugs cleared in a median 22.5 minutes against 30. The effect is largest in wider tubes, such as airway tubes. (Horne 2025.)

{{figure:W21}}

**The coated cuff.** Dip coating covers the tube and its cuff. The clip and the photograph at the top show a coated cuff in our lab, inflating and deflating.

**Insertion force, in human cadaveric cochleae.** Coated cochlear implant arrays needed about half the peak force: about 42 percent less, 64.2 against 37.0 millinewtons (Bennion 2021), and about 50 percent less, about 68 against 35 millinewtons (Peel 2024). Coated cochlear implant arrays are the published model for insertion force.

{{figure:W07}}

**Friction and bacteria.** Friction on coated silicone was more than 90 percent lower against steel, ceramic and guinea pig dermis (Bennion 2021), and at least 90 percent lower against guinea pig trachea (Peel 2024). In rats challenged with S. aureus, coated silicone carried about 155 bacteria per field at day 21, against about 940 uncoated. (Shen 2021.)

## How it fits your process

The published adhesion data are on silicone, where the film bonds so firmly that the gel tears before the silicone interface lets go (Leigh 2019). ZwiCoat has also tested bonding to PVC, the usual tube material, in its own lab. The feasibility round confirms it on your tubes. The coating is drug-free, so there is no antimicrobial agent to manage.
