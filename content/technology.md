# Technology page copy (the platform white paper, summarized)

## Opening

Every device that touches the body starts the same way: within seconds,
proteins coat it. Bacteria, cells and clot build on that film, and
depending on the device the result is a scar capsule, an infection, a
blockage or a lost signal. ZwiGuard is a hydration barrier that resists
that first step. It is not a drug.

## How it works (one hand-drawn SVG diagram, no cartoon)

ZwiGuard is a zwitterionic hydrogel built from sulfobetaine and
carboxybetaine methacrylate. Each repeat unit carries balanced positive
and negative charges and a strong dipole that binds a tightly ordered
shell of water. Proteins, cells and bacteria cannot displace the shell,
so they do not stick. The hold is stronger than PEG's hydrogen-bonded
hydration, and zwitterionic hydrogels implanted in mice resisted the
foreign body reaction outright in a landmark 2013 study (Zhang et al.,
Nature Biotechnology).

One UV step does two jobs. Benzophenone grafts the hydrogel covalently to
the device surface, and the same light crosslinks the network. The result
is a fully crosslinked hydrogel permanently anchored to the device: not a
thin tethered brush, not an adsorbed layer that can wash off, not an
additive blended into the polymer.

## What it does (W05, W04, W07, W08, each with its source line)

Antifouling, less scar, lower impedance, less friction: the four
properties, each with one chart and one number.

## It stays on (W06, W11, W09)

Antifouling intact after one year implanted; PEG-type films did not stay
intact. Explanted films keep their lubricity. The coating survived
re-curving of pre-curved arrays, repeated cochlear insertions, inflation
of a tissue expander to 200 percent of its volume, and a 50 J impact. It
passed 7 of 8 sterilization conditions (steam, EtO, UV and gamma wet or
dry, dry e-beam; wet e-beam failed) and bond strength held after gamma
or e-beam plus shipping. In accelerated aging at 82 C, the standard
recipe loses about 1 to 2 percent of its mass per equivalent year at
body temperature.

## Substrates (W10, W02)

Bonds to silicone, PVC, PEEK and parylene-C, and bridges platinum
contacts. PEEK needs no primer; its own backbone acts as the
photoinitiator, and coated PEEK fails inside the gel rather than at the
interface. PVC bonds without benzophenone at all. Polyurethane,
titanium, cobalt-chrome and PTFE are not yet tested; the feasibility
round answers that on your parts.

## Process (video slot, W15, W03)

Dip coating with an automated jig, or overmolding and printed molds for
full encapsulation and curved arrays. Thickness is tunable from about 1
to 100 micrometers by cure time and formulation (on ET tubes, about 250
to 375 micrometers at 9 to 15 s). Materials cost under a dollar per
device. No nitrogen handling for most products. A process database of
468 coated implant samples, each with its settings, photographs and a
score, is how we tightened the process: the median coating score rose
from 55 to 78 across three jig generations.

## Compared with other coatings (table from the GEN white paper)

Hydrophilic (lubricious): lowers friction; several dry out or wash off;
FDA has documented peeling and shedding. Biocidal and drug-eluting:
finite reservoir; silver 12.5 vs 12.6 percent CAUTI; antibiotic cement
did not change one-year infection revision; resistance and
hypersensitivity concerns; added FDA testing. ZwiGuard: binds a water
shell; covalently grafted and crosslinked; antifouling intact after 12
months implanted; drug-free, non-biocidal; Master File supports each
submission.

## Regulatory, in one paragraph

A coated device usually needs a new 510(k) or a PMA supplement. Our
Device Master File holds the formulation, process and safety data; FDA
reviews it confidentially and each submission incorporates it by
reference. We are building an ISO 13485 quality system under our Phase
II program. Zwitterionic surfaces have been cleared before (a PICC in
2012, a Foley series from 2020). Performance claims rest on bench data;
clinical claims need clinical data; ZwiGuard is non-biocidal, so no
antimicrobial-agent data are required.
