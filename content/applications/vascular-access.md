# Vascular access devices

Source: VA white paper; MedTech tubes deck.

## Spec strip

- Devices: PICCs, CVCs, midlines, implanted ports, dialysis catheters
- Substrates with archived tests: silicone, PVC (polyurethane: feasibility
  round)
- Coverage: outer wall and lumen in one dip step
- Thickness: about 1 to 100 micrometers (grafted brushes run about 2)
- Sterilization: steam, EtO, gamma, dry e-beam
- Regulatory path: traditional 510(k) with Pre-Sub; zwitterionic PICC
  (K113225) and Foley (K192034 to K233013) already cleared
- Evidence level: in vitro, small animal; blood-contact screen in joint
  development

## The problem

Within seconds of insertion plasma protein coats both walls of a
catheter. Inside, it seeds fibrin sheath and occlusion: 12 percent of
PICCs occluded in one multihospital study, and dysfunction removes up to
20 percent of dialysis catheters. Outside, it gives thrombus a base and
staphylococci a foothold; a single CLABSI costs about $45,800, and PICCs
carry 2.5 times the DVT odds of other central lines. Today's fixes act
after the film forms and wear off: antimicrobial CVCs cut bloodstream
infection about 2 percent absolute, and in the PICNIC trial neither a
hydrophobic nor a chlorhexidine PICC beat plain polyurethane (the
chlorhexidine arm had the most complications).

## What ZwiGuard does here

- About 95 percent less fibrinogen and fibroblast adhesion on coated
  silicone (Leigh 2019); 88 percent fewer fibroblasts in a separate study
  (Horne 2023).
- 97 percent less S. aureus and 92 percent less S. epidermidis adhesion
  (Shen 2021); those two species cause 45 percent of dialysis bloodstream
  infections.
- Antifouling intact after one year implanted; capsule 27 micrometers
  against 82 uncoated (Horne 2023).
- More than 90 percent lower friction against tissue, steel and ceramic
  (Bennion 2021).
- Lumens: the hydrogel grafts inside narrow tubes and improved clearance
  of a sticky biological fluid (Horne 2025).
- Precedent: a grafted sulfobetaine catheter surface cut thrombus about
  99 percent in a canine model (Smith 2012), and FDA cleared the PICC.

## Why it drops in

One dip step covers inside and out on existing cleared designs, no new
molds, materials under a dollar per device. Drug-free: nothing to load,
stabilize or deplete, no selective pressure, no chlorhexidine
hypersensitivity, no combination product.

## How to start

Send catheters; we coat them free and return coated parts, matched
controls and data on coverage, adhesion, friction and protein and
bacterial adhesion. Your team runs flow and blood-contact tests on the
coated parts; a formal hemocompatibility screen is part of joint
development.
