# Indwelling catheters: urinary and drainage

Source: IC white paper; MedTech tubes deck.

## Spec strip

- Devices: Foley and intermittent catheters, ureteral stents, surgical
  drains
- Substrates with archived tests: silicone, PVC (polyurethane: feasibility
  round)
- Thickness: about 1 to 100 micrometers
- Sterilization: steam, EtO, gamma, dry e-beam
- Regulatory path: traditional 510(k); a zwitterion-modified silicone
  Foley is already cleared (K192034 through K233013)
- Evidence level: in vitro, small animal; urine, encrustation and
  Gram-negative screens in joint development

## The problem

About 75 percent of hospital urinary infections are catheter-associated
and bacteriuria develops at 3 to 10 percent per day. Blockage is the
long-term failure: none of 18 catheter types, including hydrogel- and
silver-coated ones, resisted Proteus encrustation in a bladder model, and
48 percent of long-term users had a blockage within 12 weeks. Silver has
not changed the picture: in the CATHETER trial (7,102 patients) symptomatic
infection was 12.6 percent with standard catheters and 12.5 percent with
silver alloy.

## What ZwiGuard does here

- About 95 percent less protein and fibroblast adhesion; 97 and 92
  percent less staphylococcal adhesion on coated silicone (Leigh 2019,
  Shen 2021).
- Antifouling intact after one year in vivo (Horne 2023).
- More than 90 percent lower friction, and lubricity is the cleared label
  claim on the zwitterion Foley already on the market (Bennion 2021; FDA
  510(k) summaries).
- Dip coating grafts the lumen as well as the outer wall, and improved
  mucus clearance inside tubes 2.6 mm and wider (Horne 2025).
- Not yet measured: urine, encrustation and Gram-negative adhesion. The
  feasibility round measures adhesion and friction on your catheter; your
  team runs the bladder-model and urine tests on the coated parts.
