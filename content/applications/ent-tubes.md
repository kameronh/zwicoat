# ENT and feeding tubes

Source: Horne 2025 (mucus flow); MedTech tubes deck slides 9 to 11.

## Spec strip

- Devices: tympanostomy tubes, feeding tubes, sinus and ear drains
- Substrates with archived tests: silicone, PVC
- Coverage: lumen and outer wall in one dip step; inner-wall coating
  down to 1.0 mm openings
- Sterilization: steam, EtO, gamma, dry e-beam
- Evidence level: benchtop lumen flow and plug clearance (published)

## The problem

About 667,000 US children get ear tubes each year and about one in five
need a second set; tubes clog and drain. Feeding tubes clog and colonize
and stomas get infected; today's fixes are flushing and replacing the
tube.

## What ZwiGuard does here

- Saline and 5 percent mucus flow at about 0 degrees of tilt on coated
  sheets against 40 to 45 degrees on silicone; 10 percent mucus needs
  under 10 degrees against 30. (Horne 2025)
- In tubes, coated 2.64 mm tubes started flow at 31 degrees against 48
  (p = 0.002), and coated 1.0 mm tubes beat even a larger 1.6 mm uncoated
  tube. (Horne 2025)
- Dried mucus plugs cleared in a median 22.5 minutes against 30 for
  uncoated tubes, with early clearances within 3 minutes. (Horne 2025)
- 97 and 92 percent less staphylococcal adhesion in vitro (Shen 2021).

## Why it drops in

Dip coating grafts the inside of a tube in the same step as the outside.
The benefit shrinks with diameter as capillary forces take over, so a
coating buys flow performance without upsizing the tube.
