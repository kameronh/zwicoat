# Neural interfaces: cochlear implants, neurostimulators, BCI

Source: ZwiGuard Neurostimulator/BCI white paper; Ben-Shlomo 2026;

- Substrates with archived tests: implant silicone, platinum contacts,

- Evidence level: large animal (sheep, functional human arrays), small

## The problem

A neural electrode fails at one place: the interface. Within seconds of
implantation proteins coat the array, and over weeks a fibrotic and glial
capsule walls it off. For an electrode that capsule is an electrical
problem. Impedance rises, so stimulation needs more voltage and power.
Current spreads, so channels lose selectivity. Recording channels go
quiet as scar isolates them. Geometry changes and drug elution help at
the margins; none of them change the surface where the cascade starts.

## What ZwiGuard does here

- **Thinner capsule on a real array.** Human cochlear implant arrays
  coated with ZwiGuard and implanted in mice for one year had a capsule
  about 60 percent thinner (about 47 to 18 micrometers), over the
  platinum contacts as well as the silicone. The film was intact at one
  year. (Horne 2023, Acta Biomaterialia)
- **Lower impedance on working implants.** In sheep implanted with
  functional human arrays, one coated and one uncoated ear each, total
  electrode impedance was about 67 percent lower at implantation and
  about 47 percent lower after four weeks. Access resistance, the
  clinical marker of fibrosis, was about half. (Ben-Shlomo 2026, Hearing
  Research) A separate six-month cohort shows 47 to 55 percent reductions
  across all impedance components (manuscript in preparation).
- **Channels stay separate.** Transimpedance interaction matrices on
  coated arrays stay diagonal; the coating does not short adjacent
  contacts or broaden current spread. (Ben-Shlomo 2026)
- **Less inflammation in the cochlea.** Cell density, macrophage density
  and scala tympani fibrosis were all significantly lower in the coated
  ear at the cochlear base. (Ben-Shlomo 2026)
- **Gentler insertion.** Maximum insertion force into human cadaveric
  cochleae fell about 40 percent (64 to 37 mN), with smoother force
  traces. (Bennion 2021, Otology and Neurotology)
- **Bridges metal.** The crosslinked film spans platinum contacts while
  anchored to the surrounding polymer, so high-surface-area metals can be
  used without the foreign body reaction a bare metal would provoke.

## Why it drops in

One UV step grafts and crosslinks the film on silicone, parylene-C and
PVC and across platinum. A printed mold matched to the array gives a
uniform 25 micrometer film; dip coating handles straight arrays and
leads. No nitrogen handling, materials under a dollar per device, and
the coating survives the sterilization cycles the devices already use.
