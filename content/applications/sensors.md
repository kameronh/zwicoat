# Implanted and indwelling sensors (CGM)

Source: MedTech sensors deck; literature cited there (McClatchey 2019,
Xie 2018).

## Spec strip

- Devices: continuous glucose monitors, implanted biosensors
- Substrates with archived tests: silicone, PVC, parylene-C, platinum
  (sensor membranes: feasibility round)
- Thickness: about 1 to 100 micrometers
- Sterilization: EtO, gamma, dry e-beam
- Evidence level: small animal (capsule, one year) on silicone;
  zwitterionic-coating sensor precedent in the literature

## The problem

The body's response to the sensor limits accuracy and wear life. In mice,
over 80 percent of CGM signal delay came from the fibrous capsule around
the sensor. Labeled wear life is the race, and the capsule is the limit.

## What ZwiGuard does here

- A thinner capsule: 27 against 82 micrometers on silicone in mice at six
  weeks, about 60 percent thinner on implanted arrays at one year (Horne
  2023).
- Antifouling unchanged after six months in vivo (Horne 2023).
- Precedent for the class: a zwitterionic (pMPC) coating cut CGM error
  from 73 to 17 percent in mice and 48 to 18 percent in primates, with
  no recalibration (Xie 2018, Nat. Biomed. Eng.). ZwiGuard differs in
  being a thick, crosslinked, covalently grafted hydrogel rather than a
  brush.
- Not yet measured: glucose diffusion through the film and sensor
  response time. The feasibility round coats your sensor and returns
  response data from your own test.

## Why it drops in

One coating step; thickness tunable down to a few micrometers where
diffusion matters. Drug-free.

## How to start
