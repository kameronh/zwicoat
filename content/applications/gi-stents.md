# Gastrointestinal stents

Source: GI white paper.

## Spec strip

- Devices: covered biliary, esophageal, duodenal and colonic stents;
  plastic biliary stents; feeding tubes
- Substrates with archived tests: silicone, PVC (polyurethane, PTFE, FEP:
  feasibility round)
- Thickness: about 1 to 100 micrometers
- Sterilization: steam, EtO, gamma, dry e-beam
- Regulatory path: Class II, 510(k) (esophageal ESW, duodenal MUM,
  colonic MQR, biliary FGE); Pre-Sub recommended
- Evidence level: in vitro, small animal, benchtop lumen flow

## The problem

Soon after placement a protein and mucus film forms on the stent and
bacteria, sludge and tissue build on it. Covered metal stents trade one
failure for another: in 12 studies of 2,358 patients they had more sludge
(OR 3.25) and more migration (OR 7.92) than uncovered stents. In the
esophagus, 29 percent of 216 patients had recurrent dysphagia from
overgrowth, migration or food bolus. Hydrophilic and PTFE plastic stents
did not extend patency over polyethylene in three randomized trials, and
a 2007 review judged silver and antimicrobial stents not clinically
relevant enough to replace conventional ones. Slicker alone has not been
enough; the surface has to resist the film itself.

## What ZwiGuard does here

- About 95 percent less fibrinogen and fibroblast adhesion; 97 and 92
  percent less staphylococcal adhesion (Leigh 2019, Shen 2021).
- Antifouling intact after one year in vivo (Horne 2023).
- Mucus flows at a lower tilt on coated surfaces: about 0 degrees on
  coated sheets against 40 to 45 uncoated, and 31 against 48 degrees in
  2.6 mm tubes; dried mucus plugs cleared faster in saline (median 22.5
  vs 30 minutes). (Horne 2025)
- More than 90 percent lower friction (Bennion 2021): a reason to test
  delivery, not a patency claim.

## Why it drops in

One coating step on existing coverings and plastic stents. Bonds to
silicone and polyurethane coverings without a primer; PTFE and FEP
coverings and nitinol are feasibility-round questions, as is crimping and
expansion of a finished stent.
